# Part 1 — What the device actually does

A proposal for settlement is a **fee-shifting sanction for unreasonably rejecting a settlement offer**. You serve a written proposal; if the other side rejects it and then does sufficiently worse at trial, they pay your fees and costs **from the date the proposal was served**.

Four features drive everything else:

**1. Entitlement is arithmetic, not equitable.** Once the numbers line up, the trial court has no discretion to deny fees on the ground that the rejection was reasonable. [*TGI Friday's, Inc. v. Dvorak*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1715682/tgi-fridays-inc-v-dvorak/), 663 So. 2d 606 (Fla. 1995), adopting [*Schmidt v. Fortner*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1676218/schmidt-v-fortner/), 629 So. 2d 1036 (Fla. 4th DCA 1993). The court's discretion goes to the **amount**, not the entitlement.

**2. There is exactly one escape hatch, and it is narrow.** § 768.79(8)(a) (2024; **(7)(a)** in pre-2022 sources) lets the court disallow an award on a finding that the proposal **was not made in good faith**. The reasonableness factors at (8)(b) go to the *amount*. See [Part 7.D](#the-good-faith-challenge).

**3. You do not have to win.** § 768.79 is **not a prevailing-party statute**. [*Coates v. R.J. Reynolds Tobacco Co.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406669/brinda-coates-etc-v-rj-reynolds-tobacco-company/), 365 So. 3d 353 (Fla. 2023). A defendant can recover fees even where the plaintiff takes a substantial judgment, so long as the judgment falls 25% below the defendant's proposal.

**4. The requirements live in two instruments, and both bind you.** The substantive right is in **§ 768.79**; the procedure is in **rule 1.442**. Compliance with one is not compliance with the other — the classic casualty is a proposal that tracks the rule perfectly and never cites the statute. [*Campbell v. Goldman*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140885/campbell-v-goldman/), 959 So. 2d 223 (Fla. 2007).

### What the fee award is worth

| Included | Authority |
|---|---|
| Reasonable attorney's fees **and** costs incurred **from the date of service** of the proposal | § 768.79(1) |
| **Appellate** fees | [*Frosti v. Creel*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/), 979 So. 2d 912 (Fla. 2008); [*Coates*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406669/brinda-coates-etc-v-rj-reynolds-tobacco-company/) |
| Fees even though your client lost the case outright | [*Coates*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406669/brinda-coates-etc-v-rj-reynolds-tobacco-company/) |

| Excluded | Authority |
|---|---|
| Any **contingency risk multiplier** | [*Sarkis v. Allstate Ins. Co.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1831641/sarkis-v-allstate-ins-co/), 863 So. 2d 210 (Fla. 2003) |
| Fees incurred **before** service | § 768.79(1) |

⚠️ **Do not aggregate.** If you served several separate proposals, you may not sum them and compare the total against the judgment. [*Anderson v. Hilton Hotels Corp.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/), 202 So. 3d 846 (Fla. 2016). Each proposal stands or falls on its own.

# Part 2 — The decision to serve

## A. Plaintiff-side

**Threshold:** the judgment obtained must be **at least 25% greater** than your demand.

**Price the demand off the *judgment*, not the verdict.** "Judgment obtained" is a term of art (Part 8) — it is the net judgment **plus** your fees, taxable costs, and prejudgment interest **as of the date of the offer**. On an older, heavily-worked file, those accrued items can carry a demand over the threshold that the raw verdict would not.

**Serve early, but not too early.** Fees run from service, so an early proposal maximizes exposure. But the earlier you serve, the less you know about value — and a demand that turns out to be 25% *below* four-fifths of the judgment is simply money left on the table.

**⚠️ Setoffs can be decisive.** Because the comparison is arithmetic, a setoff of a few hundred dollars can eliminate entitlement outright. In [*Rollins v. Pizzarelli*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1770539/rollins-v-pizzarelli/), 761 So. 2d 294 (Fla. 2000), a PIP setoff moved a $25,048 judgment to $24,523.22 and destroyed the fee claim on a $20,000 demand. **Model your comparator net of every setoff you can foresee** — PIP deductibles, collateral sources, [*Fabre*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1617274/fabre-v-marin/) allocations, prior settlements.

## B. Defendant-side

**Threshold:** the judgment is **one of no liability**, or the judgment obtained by the plaintiff is **at least 25% less** than your proposal.

A defense verdict satisfies the threshold automatically. That has been true only since **October 1, 1990** — before ch. 90-119 § 48, the statute measured against "the judgment obtained *by the plaintiff*," so defendants could not recover on a defense verdict. Irrelevant to a modern file; relevant if you are dealing with an old accrual date (Part 10.D).

**The nominal proposal is legal but untested.** Nothing squarely forbids a $1 proposal, and one went unchallenged in [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/). But the Court flagged doubt about "a one dollar offer of settlement" as early as [*Leapai v. Milton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1769501/leapai-v-milton/), 595 So. 2d 12 (Fla. 1992) (n.2), and **no decision in the Supreme Court corpus squarely resolves a good-faith challenge**. Treat a nominal proposal as carrying real, undecided risk — see [Part 12.A](#a-good-faith-is-the-least-developed-area-in-the-regime).

**Differentiated proposals among codefendants are legitimate.** [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) records the ordinary strategic reasons approvingly: to force one defendant to settle, to "eliminate the defendant for whom the jury may have sympathy," or to remove the defendant with the best lawyer. And **motive is irrelevant to facial validity** — it goes to good faith only. [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/).

## C. The cost of getting it wrong

Two asymmetries worth putting in front of a client:

- An **invalid** proposal costs you the fee claim you were building — but it does not otherwise hurt you.
- **Rejecting** a valid proposal that you then fail to beat by the margin can transfer the entire post-service cost of the litigation. On a case tried two years after service, that is usually the largest single number in the file.

The Court itself has said the device increases litigation rather than reducing it. [*Attorneys' Title Ins. Fund, Inc. v. Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/), 36 So. 3d 646, 650 (Fla. 2010): the intended effect "has been in sharp contrast to the intended outcome." Budget for a fight over validity.

# Part 3 — The calendar

## A. The service window — rule 1.442(b)

| Event | Earliest | Latest |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal **to a defendant** | 90 days after **service of process on that defendant** | 45 days before the trial date or the first day of the trial docket, **whichever is earlier** |
| Proposal **to a plaintiff** | 90 days after **the action was commenced** | same |

⚠️ **Mediation does not move these dates.** Rule 1.442(j). The Legislature tried to alter the timing through § 44.102(6) and the Court struck the attempt as purely procedural. [*Knealing v. Puleo*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1725782/knealing-v-puleo/), 675 So. 2d 593 (Fla. 1996).

⚠️ **A continuance changes the outside date.** The 45-day limit runs off the trial setting, so a reset trial date reopens the window. Diary it against the *current* order setting trial, not the original one.

## B. The acceptance clock — rule 1.442(f)(1)

- **30 days.** A proposal is **deemed rejected** unless a written acceptance is delivered within 30 days of service.
- 🔴 **The extra-days-for-service provision does not apply.** Rule 1.442(f)(1) expressly excludes Fla. R. Jud. Admin. **2.514(b)**. Thirty days means thirty days — no five-day add-on, whatever the method of service.
- 🔴 **A motion to enlarge does not toll the clock.** [*Koppel v. Ochoa*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4498881/donna-koppel-v-laura-ochoa/), 243 So. 3d 886 (Fla. 2018), disapproving [*Goldy v. Corbett Cranes*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7767343/goldy-v-corbett-cranes-services-inc/) after nineteen years of contrary practice. The offeree in [*Koppel*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4498881/donna-koppel-v-laura-ochoa/) filed one day before expiry, got a hearing two months later, and purported to accept on day 90. It did not work.
- **No oral communication** constitutes acceptance, rejection, or counteroffer. Rule 1.442(f)(1).

## C. The fee-motion deadline — rule 1.442(g) and rule 1.525

**30 days**, and the two instruments measure from different events:

| Instrument | Runs from |
|---|---|
| **Rule 1.442(g)** — **jury** action | **Return of the verdict** |
| **Rule 1.442(g)** — **nonjury** action | **Entry of judgment** |
| § 768.79 | Entry of judgment |

**The rule prevails where it differs from the statute**, because the timing is procedural. [*Gulliver Academy, Inc. v. Bodek*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1843592/gulliver-academy-inc-v-bodek/), 694 So. 2d 675 (Fla. 1997). **In a jury trial, calendar from the verdict.**

See Part 9 for what the 30 days does and does not tolerate.

# Part 4 — 🔴 Joint proposals

**This is where practitioners lose most often.** Read this part even if you think your proposal is not joint.

## A. The rule

**Rule 1.442(c)(3):** a proposal may be made by or to any party or parties and any combination of them, but **"[a] joint proposal must state the amount and terms attributable to each party."**

**Rule 1.442(c)(4)** is the one exception: where a party is alleged to be **solely vicariously, constructively, derivatively, or technically liable**, a joint proposal made by or served on that party need not apportion as to that party. Adopted in 2010 to undo [*Lamb v. Matetzschk*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1950924/lamb-v-matetzschk/), 906 So. 2d 1037 (Fla. 2005).

## B. When is a proposal "joint"? — the operative test

⭐ **A proposal becomes joint when it *speaks for* or *binds* another party — not merely because another party has claims pending.** [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) (2016) and [*Allen v. Nunez*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4540927/w-riley-allen-v-jairo-rafael-nunez/), 258 So. 3d 1207 (Fla. 2018).

The line is drawn precisely by comparing two cases:

| | [*Audiffred v. Arnold*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794251/valerie-audiffred-v-thomas-b-arnold/), 161 So. 3d 1274 (Fla. 2015) | [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) (2016) |
|---|---|---|
| Recital | resolves "[a]ny and all claims **Plaintiffs** have brought" | "PLAINTIFF," defined as **Troy Anderson alone** |
| Required of the non-offeror spouse | **both** spouses had to dismiss | never mentioned |
| Result | **joint** — apportionment required, proposal invalid | **not joint** — "[a]pportionment simply was not an issue" |

**Practical rule: police your defined terms and your dismissal language.** A plural noun, an undefined "Plaintiffs," or a release/dismissal clause that reaches a non-offeror converts a single-party proposal into a joint one that must apportion.

Two further boundary points:

- **A single offeror can make a "joint" proposal** if it resolves claims by or against parties who are neither offerors nor offerees. [*Audiffred*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794251/valerie-audiffred-v-thomas-b-arnold/).
- **A proposal captioned from one entity but written in the plural is joint.** [*Pratt v. Weiss*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794254/ancel-pratt-jr-v-michael-c-weiss-do/), 161 So. 3d 1268 (Fla. 2015) — a limited partnership and its general partner are distinct entities, and the text controlled over the caption.

## C. Apportionment is required even when it makes no sense

- "**Even where no logical apportionment can be made, it is nonetheless required** where more than one offeror or offeree is involved." [*Pratt*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794254/ancel-pratt-jr-v-michael-c-weiss-do/), 161 So. 3d at 1272–73. Difficulty, coextensive liability, and the ability to apportion after acceptance are "not recognized legal elements to disregard or circumvent the rule."
- "It may take some **creative drafting**." [*Lamb*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1950924/lamb-v-matetzschk/).
- 🔴 **Required even for a unified, single claim held by spouses.** [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) (Fla. July 2, 2026), 4–3 — **subject to the pending rehearing motion**. The proposal there was a joint, unapportioned **$10,000** from a husband and wife; the verdict was **$41,273.70**; the fee claim failed on (c)(3).

**Spouses are the recurring casualty.** [*Allstate Indemnity Co. v. Hingson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1860954/allstate-indem-co-v-hingson/), 808 So. 2d 197 (Fla. 2002); [*D'Angelo v. Fitzmaurice*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1831568/dangelo-v-fitzmaurice/), 863 So. 2d 311 (Fla. 2003); [*Audiffred*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794251/valerie-audiffred-v-thomas-b-arnold/); [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) — four sets of spouses, plus the [*Herzogs*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1727784/herzog-v-k-mart-corp/) in the case [*Hingson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1860954/allstate-indem-co-v-hingson/) disapproved. **There is no spousal-unity exception.** Pariente, C.J., objected in 2005 that the rule as construed means "a joint offer to a husband and wife is no longer authorized" ([*Lamb*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1950924/lamb-v-matetzschk/), specially concurring); the objection has never been adopted.

## D. Conditioning acceptance — what is and is not allowed

🔴 **One offeror → multiple offerees, conditioned on all of them accepting: INVALID.** [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/) (2010). "An offer that cannot be unilaterally accepted to create a binding settlement is an **illusory offer**" — it divests each offeree of independent control.

✅ **Multiple offerors → one offeree, conditioned on that offeree accepting as to all offerors: approved by the district courts.** The **"all-or-nothing allocated offer."** [*Hoang Dinh Duong v. Ziadie*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5004631/hoang-dinh-duong-v-ziadie/), 153 So. 3d 354, 358–60 (Fla. 4th DCA 2014), collecting [*Wolfe v. Culpepper Constructors*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4987556/wolfe-v-culpepper-constructors-inc/) (2d DCA 2012), [*Rossmore v. Smith*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5048550/rossmore-v-smith/) (5th DCA 2011), [*Andrews v. Frey*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2498180/andrews-v-frey/) (5th DCA 2011), and [*Donovan Marine v. Delmonico*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2532139/donovan-marine-inc-v-delmonico/) (4th DCA 2010).

The distinction is **structural**, and it is why [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/) does not reach the second structure:

| | [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/) | All-or-nothing allocated offer |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | one offeror → **multiple offerees** | **multiple offerors** → one offeree |
| Condition | all **offerees** must accept | the single offeree must accept as to all **offerors** |
| Independent control | destroyed | intact — there is only one decision-maker |
| Result | **invalid** | **valid** |

⚠️ **Two cautions before you rely on the collection.** [*Andrews v. Frey*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2498180/andrews-v-frey/) was later disapproved by the Supreme Court in [*Audiffred*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2794251/valerie-audiffred-v-thomas-b-arnold/) — on a different question (sole-offeror vs. joint proposal), not on the all-or-nothing structure, but say so if you cite it. And the Supreme Court has not itself passed on the structure.

⚠️ **The statutory carve-out is narrow.** § 768.79(6) (2022) permits a conditional joint offer only where a **property insurer** makes it in a **breach of contract action**. It is not a general relaxation of [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/), and it is **not** an apportionment provision. Do not describe it as one.

## E. ⚠️ The price of an all-or-nothing allocated offer: the 25% test becomes conjunctive

This is the most under-appreciated consequence in the field, and it is stated plainly in [*Hoang Dinh Duong*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5004631/hoang-dinh-duong-v-ziadie/):

> if the verdict for **any** claimant was not 25% higher than that claimant's allocated share, **none** of the claimants recovers fees. "[I]t was not enough that the total amount of the verdict exceed the total amount of the offer by twenty-five percent."

**Allocate deliberately.** Loading one claimant heavily to make the aggregate look reasonable can hand the offeree a defense: if that claimant underperforms at trial, the whole fee claim fails, however well the others did.

# Part 5 — The drafting checklist

Each row is a requirement and the decision that enforces it. Requirements marked 🔴 have killed fee claims in the Supreme Court.

## A. Threshold requirements

| # | Requirement | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **In writing** | rule 1.442(c)(1) | |
| 2 | 🔴 **Cite § 768.79 itself** — not just rule 1.442 | § 768.79(2)(a); rule 1.442(c)(1); [*Campbell*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140885/campbell-v-goldman/) | Citing only the rule is **fatal**. The statute requires the proposal to "state that it is being made pursuant to this section." Belt and braces: cite both. |
| 3 | **Name the party making it and the party to whom it is made** | rule 1.442(c)(2)(A) | Use defined terms. Define them in the singular unless you intend a joint proposal. |

## B. Rule 1.442(c)(2) content requirements

| Subdiv. | Requirement | Drafting note |
|---|---|---|
| **(A)** | Name the offeror and the offeree | See above |
| **(B)** | State that it **resolves all damages** that would otherwise be awarded in final judgment | An **unqualified** proposal resolves every pending claim, counterclaims included — § 768.79(2); [*MGR Equipment Corp. v. Wilson Ice Enterprises*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1846806/mgr-equip-v-wilson-ice-enterprises/), 731 So. 2d 1262 (Fla. 1999). If you intend something narrower, say so with particularity, and understand you are creating a "condition" under (C). |
| **(C)** | State **with particularity any relevant conditions** | A **general release is a condition** and must be stated with particularity. [*State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Nichols*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1697927/state-farm-mut-auto-ins-co-v-nichols/), 932 So. 2d 1067 (Fla. 2006). |
| **(D)** | State the **total amount** and **all nonmonetary terms** with particularity | Every nonmonetary term is a place to lose. |
| **(E)** | State with particularity the amount proposed to settle a **punitive damages** claim, if any | Required only if punitive damages are claimed. |
| **(F)** | State whether it **includes attorney's fees** and whether fees **are part of the legal claim** | 🔴 Invalid for omission **where the complaint actually pleaded a fee claim** — [*Diamond Aircraft Industries, Inc. v. Horowitch*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/), 107 So. 3d 362 (Fla. 2013). **Excused** where fees were not sought in the pleadings — [*Kuhajda v. Borden Dairy Co.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4314140/susanne-l-kuhajda-v-borden-dairy-company-of-alabama-llc/), 202 So. 3d 391 (Fla. 2016). **Always include it anyway**; the exception depends on facts you cannot control. |
| **(G)** | **Certificate of service** | See Part 6. |

## C. The particularity standard — [*Nichols*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1697927/state-farm-mut-auto-ins-co-v-nichols/)

The operative test, and the one the trial court will apply:

> A proposal must be "**sufficiently clear and definite to allow the offeree to make an informed decision without needing clarification**." The rule "does not demand the impossible." **"If ambiguity within the proposal could reasonably affect the offeree's decision, the proposal will not satisfy the particularity requirement."**

⭐ **And the qualifier that saves most releases:** "**The rule aims to prevent ambiguity, not breadth.**" A sweeping general release is fine if it is unambiguous. Summarize it if you like — a summary suffices "as long as it eliminates any reasonable ambiguity about its scope."

**Two rules of construction that work in the drafter's favor:**

- **The proposal is read as a whole.** An arguably loose recital in one paragraph is cured by the rest of the document. [*Allen v. Nunez*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4540927/w-riley-allen-v-jairo-rafael-nunez/).
- **Courts are discouraged from "nitpicking."** [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) and [*Allen*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4540927/w-riley-allen-v-jairo-rafael-nunez/) both reversed district courts for manufacturing ambiguity, in the same words: the courts below "**unnecessarily injected ambiguity into these proceedings and created more judicial labor, not less**."
- **A transmittal letter is not a second, conflicting proposal.** Reading it as one is "grammatically possible" but "substantively unreasonable." [*Hoang Dinh Duong*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5004631/hoang-dinh-duong-v-ziadie/). Still — do not put settlement terms in the cover letter.

## D. Attach and align

- If a release is referenced, **attach it**. Do not incorporate a document the offeree has not seen.
- Check that the release's scope, the (c)(2)(B) all-damages recital, and any (c)(2)(C) conditions do not contradict each other.
- Check that no term reaches a party who is not named as an offeror or offeree — that is what converts the proposal to a joint one (Part 4.B).

# Part 6 — Serving the proposal

| Point | Authority |
|---|---|
| Proposals are **served, not filed**, unless filing is necessary to enforce the rule | rule 1.442(d) |
| **Premature filing is not fatal.** The remedy is a motion to strike, with leave to refile | [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/) |
| ⭐ **Proposals are NOT subject to the e-mail service requirements of Fla. R. Jud. Admin. 2.516** — a proposal is neither a pleading nor a document filed in a court proceeding | [*Wheaton v. Wheaton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4581389/sandra-kent-wheaton-v-mardella-wheaton/), 261 So. 3d 1236 (Fla. 2019) |
| The 30-day acceptance period gets **no extra days for the method of service** | rule 1.442(f)(1) (excluding rule 2.514(b)) |

⚠️ **Practical advice notwithstanding [*Wheaton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4581389/sandra-kent-wheaton-v-mardella-wheaton/): serve by e-mail in conformity with rule 2.516 anyway, and keep the certificate.** [*Wheaton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4581389/sandra-kent-wheaton-v-mardella-wheaton/)'s primary holding is that 2.516 does not apply; its alternative holding — that noncompliance would be excused because the proposal satisfied § 768.79's substantive requirements — applies a substantive-compliance test broader than anything in [*Kuhajda*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4314140/susanne-l-kuhajda-v-borden-dairy-company-of-alabama-llc/), and the Supreme Court has **not revisited it since 2019**. [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) does not mention it. Do not build a strategy on the alternative holding.

**Prove service.** Include the (c)(2)(G) certificate, keep the transmission record, and calendar day 30 the moment it goes out.

# Part 7 — Receiving a proposal

## A. Calendar first, analyze second

Day 1 is service. Day 30 is the deadline. **No add-on days. No tolling by motion.** [*Koppel*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4498881/donna-koppel-v-laura-ochoa/). Put the date in the file, on the calendar, and in the client letter the same day.

## B. Deciding

Model the comparator honestly, using the [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula (Part 8) — not the verdict you hope for. On a plaintiff-side file, ask: is there a realistic verdict at which the **judgment obtained**, computed as of today, falls below 75% of this proposal? On a defense file: above 125% of the demand?

⚠️ **Accepting a proposal silent as to fees ends the fee claim too.** An accepted offer of judgment that says nothing about attorney's fees **terminates the litigation** and forecloses a later motion for statutory fees; statutory fee rights **can be waived**. [*Unicare Health Facilities, Inc. v. Mort*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1670587/unicare-health-facilities-inc-v-mort/), 553 So. 2d 159 (Fla. 1989) (the "organic right of parties to contract a settlement, which by definition concludes all claims unless the contract of settlement specifies otherwise"). **If you have a separate fee entitlement, do not accept a silent proposal without resolving it.**

## C. Mechanics of acceptance, rejection, and withdrawal

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Acceptance** | Written notice delivered within 30 days — rule 1.442(f)(1). **A filed written acceptance forms a binding settlement contract**; acceptance is not performance. [*Suarez Trucking FL Corp. v. Souders*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8404436/suarez-trucking-fl-corp-v-adam-j-souders/), No. SC21-369 (Fla. Oct. 20, 2022): "Once a proper acceptance … is filed as specified in the statute, **that's it**: a settlement contract has been entered." |
| **The acceptance need not recite the offer's terms** | The mirror-image rule is "a rule of **consistency**. It is not … a rule of **regurgitation**." [*Suarez Trucking*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8404436/suarez-trucking-fl-corp-v-adam-j-souders/). But an acceptance must be **unqualified** — do not add terms. |
| **Rejection** | Automatic. A proposal is deemed rejected if not accepted in 30 days. You need do nothing. |
| **Counteroffer** | **Does not terminate the proposal.** [*Scope v. Fannelli*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7744665/scope-v-fannelli/), 639 So. 2d 141 (Fla. 5th DCA 1994). The original proposal remains open until written withdrawal or expiry — which cuts both ways: it means negotiating does not forfeit your right to accept, and it means an offeror who wants the proposal dead must **withdraw it in writing**. |
| **Withdrawal** | In writing, **before** a written acceptance is delivered; once withdrawn the proposal is void. Rule 1.442(e). |
| **Oral anything** | Irrelevant. Rule 1.442(f)(1). |

⚠️ **The framework "does not envision a process of negotiation."** Proposals are "by their very nature **take-it-or-leave-it propositions**." [*Suarez Trucking*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8404436/suarez-trucking-fl-corp-v-adam-j-souders/). Side conversations about terms are legally inert; only the writing counts.

## D. Attacking a proposal served on you

The grounds that have actually worked, in rough order of success rate:

1. **Failure to apportion a joint proposal** — rule 1.442(c)(3). Part 4. The largest category by a wide margin. Look first at defined terms, plural nouns, and any clause that binds a non-offeror.
2. **Conditioned on mutual acceptance by multiple offerees** — [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/). Check whether the § 768.79(6) property-insurer carve-out applies before arguing this.
3. **No citation to § 768.79** — [*Campbell*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140885/campbell-v-goldman/).
4. **Ambiguity that could reasonably affect the decision** — [*Nichols*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1697927/state-farm-mut-auto-ins-co-v-nichols/). ⚠️ But read [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) and [*Allen*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4540927/w-riley-allen-v-jairo-rafael-nunez/) first: the Court has twice reversed courts for manufacturing ambiguity, and the proposal is read **as a whole**. A nitpicking argument is now more likely to draw a rebuke than a win.
5. **(c)(2)(F) omission where fees were pleaded** — [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/). Check the complaint; if fees were not pleaded, [*Kuhajda*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4314140/susanne-l-kuhajda-v-borden-dairy-company-of-alabama-llc/) forecloses this.
6. **The action is not a "civil action for damages"** — Part 10.
7. **Good faith** — § 768.79(8)(a). See below.
8. **The comparison fails** — run the [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula yourself (Part 8) rather than accepting the movant's arithmetic. Setoffs are where these are won.

### The good-faith challenge

This is the only discretionary escape, and it is **the least-developed part of the regime** — which cuts both ways. There is little authority to cite, and little authority against you.

- **Motive is not a validity question.** Even an admission that a proposal was aimed at settling someone else's claim goes to good faith, not facial validity. [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/).
- **Differentiated and strategic offers are legitimate.** [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/), quoting [*Hess v. Walton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1842075/hess-v-walton/).
- **Nominal proposals are the open question.** [*Leapai*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1769501/leapai-v-milton/) n.2 (1992) raised doubt about "a one dollar offer of settlement" and said the issue "was neither presented nor addressed." [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/) (2008) let a $1 punitive proposal pass unchallenged. **No Supreme Court decision in the corpus squarely decides a good-faith challenge.** If you are attacking a nominal proposal, you are litigating on open ground — build the record in the trial court, because that is where the finding will be made.

# Part 8 — Running the 25% comparison

## A. The thresholds

| Offeror | Entitled if… |
|---|---|
| **Defendant** | judgment is **one of no liability**, **or** the judgment obtained by the plaintiff is **at least 25% less** than the defendant's proposal |
| **Plaintiff** | the judgment obtained is **at least 25% greater** than the plaintiff's demand |

§ 768.79(1); the post-judgment determination procedure is at **§ 768.79(7)** (2024) — **(6)** in any pre-2022 source.

## B. "Judgment obtained" is a term of art — the [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula

**Not the verdict.** [*White v. Steak and Ale of Florida, Inc.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/), 816 So. 2d 546 (Fla. 2002):

```
judgment obtained  =  net judgment for damages
                   +  attorney's fees        ┐
                   +  taxable costs          ├ as they stood ON THE DATE OF THE OFFER
                   +  prejudgment interest   ┘
```

Three refinements:

| Refinement | Authority |
|---|---|
| **Post-offer prejudgment interest is EXCLUDED** | [*CCM Condominium Ass'n v. Petri Positive Pest Control*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5092491/ccm-condominium-association-inc-etc-v-petri-positive-pest-control/), No. SC19-861 (Fla. Sept. 9, 2021) |
| **The whole net judgment counts** — you may not isolate a favorable slice (e.g. the compensatory portion) to defeat the threshold | [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/) |
| **Separate proposals may not be aggregated** and compared against the judgment | [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/) |

⚠️ **The formula has never been examined on the merits.** In [*CCM*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5092491/ccm-condominium-association-inc-etc-v-petri-positive-pest-control/), the Fourth District below said the plain meaning of § 768.79 does **not** support the formula but considered itself bound; the Supreme Court applied [*State v. Poole*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4741416/state-of-florida-v-mark-anthony-poole/)'s "clearly erroneous" standard and declined to look. Canady, C.J., dissenting, argued the formula's supporting authorities were § 627.428 cases with "no relevant textual similarity." **It is settled law and you should apply it — but it is settled on stare decisis, not on text.**

## C. Worked examples

**Simple, single claimant.** Defendant serves a $100,000 proposal. Verdict: $70,000. As of the date of the offer, plaintiff's taxable costs and prejudgment interest totaled $8,000.

```
judgment obtained = 70,000 + 8,000 = 78,000
75% of 100,000                     = 75,000
78,000 > 75,000  ──►  defendant is NOT entitled.
```

Note how the $8,000 of pre-offer accruals — not the verdict — decided it. **Compute the accruals before you set the number.**

**Unified claim, unapportioned joint proposal.** [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/): spouses served a joint, unapportioned **$10,000** proposal; the verdict was **$41,273.70** — comfortably past 125%. The fee claim still failed, on (c)(3), because the proposal was not apportioned. **Arithmetic success does not survive a drafting defect.**

**All-or-nothing allocated proposal — the conjunctive test.** [*Hoang Dinh Duong*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5004631/hoang-dinh-duong-v-ziadie/): $1,000,000 total, apportioned $900,000 / $50,000 / $50,000, served on one defendant. Each claimant's judgment had to beat that claimant's allocated share by 25%, or **no one** recovered fees. They did, and $557,452 in fees was affirmed. Run the test **per claimant**, not on the total.

# Part 9 — Claiming the fees

## A. The deadline

Serve the motion in accordance with **rule 1.525** — rule 1.442(g). **30 days.** See Part 3.C for the measuring event (verdict in a jury case).

| Point | Authority |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **A reservation of jurisdiction in the final judgment does NOT extend rule 1.525's 30 days** | [*Saia Motor Freight Line, Inc. v. Reid*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1899565/saia-motor-freight-line-inc-v-reid/), 930 So. 2d 598 (Fla. 2006) |
| **But 1.525 is a ceiling, not a window** — a motion served *before* judgment is timely | [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/), following [*Barco v. School Bd. of Pinellas County*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1825558/barco-v-school-bd-of-pinellas-county/) |
| **Prospective-only application is not available** | This line has never been applied prospectively — sought and refused in [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/), [*Saia*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1899565/saia-motor-freight-line-inc-v-reid/), and [*Koppel*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4498881/donna-koppel-v-laura-ochoa/) |

**Practical consequence: serve early.** There is no penalty for serving the motion before judgment and a fatal one for serving it late. [*Saia*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1899565/saia-motor-freight-line-inc-v-reid/) and [*Gulliver Academy*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1843592/gulliver-academy-inc-v-bodek/) were decided nine years apart by the same author on nearly the same facts, and reconcile only because rule 1.525 was adopted in between. Do not rely on a reservation of jurisdiction to save you.

## B. Pleading

⭐ **A § 768.79 fee claim is not subject to [*Stockman v. Downs*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1116540/stockman-v-downs/)'s pleading requirement**, because entitlement arises from an event occurring *during* the suit rather than from the nature of the claim. Discussed in [*Advanced Chiropractic & Rehabilitation Center, Corp. v. United Automobile Ins. Co.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2689203/advanced-chiropractic-and-rehabilitation-center-corp-v-united-automobile/), 140 So. 3d 529 (Fla. 2014), on district authority collected there. ⚠️ The Supreme Court has not squarely held this in a § 768.79 case in the read corpus — **plead the entitlement anyway** if you have any basis to; there is no cost to doing so.

## C. Amount

- **No contingency risk multiplier.** [*Sarkis*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1831641/sarkis-v-allstate-ins-co/).
- The § 768.79(8)(b) factors (2024; **(7)(b)** pre-2022) go to the **amount**, not entitlement. [*TGI Friday's*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1715682/tgi-fridays-inc-v-dvorak/)/[*Schmidt*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1676218/schmidt-v-fortner/).
- **Appellate fees are recoverable.** [*Frosti*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785275/frosti-v-creel/); [*Coates*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406669/brinda-coates-etc-v-rj-reynolds-tobacco-company/). Follow rule 9.400 and move in the appellate court.

# Part 10 — When § 768.79 does not apply at all

Check these before you spend an hour drafting.

## A. Actions seeking damages *and* equitable relief

**§ 768.79 does not apply** to an action seeking both damages and equitable relief, at least where the proposal is a **general** one resolving all claims. [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/). There is **no exception** for an equitable claim that lacks serious merit, and **pleading in the alternative does not help** — "an equitable claim in the alternative to a monetary claim is still part of the same civil action."

⭐ **The open workaround.** [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/) **expressly reserved** whether the result changes where the monetary and nonmonetary claims are pled **separately** and the proposal is **directed only to the monetary claim**. That question is still undecided. It is a real drafting opportunity and a real risk — see [Part 12.B](#b-the-diamond-aircraft-reservation).

## B. Contractual choice of another state's law

**§ 768.79 is substantive for conflict-of-law purposes.** Where the parties contracted to be bound by another state's substantive law, **§ 768.79 does not apply**. [*Southeast Floating Docks, Inc. v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5056323/southeast-floating-docks-inc-v-auto-owners-insurance-co/), 82 So. 3d 73 (Fla. 2012). Check the contract before you serve.

## C. Voluntary dismissal without prejudice

Fees follow only where a plaintiff's voluntary dismissal is **with prejudice**, or is a **second** voluntary dismissal. [*MX Investments, Inc. v. Crawford*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1799433/mx-investments-inc-v-crawford/), 700 So. 2d 640 (Fla. 1997). A rule 1.420(d) cost judgment is not a "judgment" for this purpose.

## D. Old accrual dates

**§ 768.79 does not reach causes of action accruing before July 1, 1986** (§ 768.71(2)), and **the applicable version is the one in effect when the cause of action accrued** — not when the offer was served. [*Metropolitan Dade County v. Jones Boatyard, Inc.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1925102/metropolitan-dade-county-v-jones-boatyard-inc/), 611 So. 2d 512 (Fla. 1993).

⚠️ **§ 45.061 was never repealed.** It was repealed *prospectively* in 1990 as to causes accruing after October 1, 1990, and survives on the books as § 45.061, Fla. Stat., for pre-October-1990 accruals. Vestigial, but if you are litigating a very old claim, check it — its § 45.061(2)(b) definition of "the amount of the judgment" differs from the [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula, and its temporal trigger is **the making of the offer**, not accrual.

## E. Things that are *not* exclusions

- **PIP and other contractual-damages suits are within the statute.** "[N]othing in the offer of judgment statute exempts claims for contractual damages." [*Nichols*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1697927/state-farm-mut-auto-ins-co-v-nichols/).
- **You need not prevail.** [*Coates*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406669/brinda-coates-etc-v-rj-reynolds-tobacco-company/).
- **A pending counterclaim does not defeat an unqualified proposal** — it is resolved by it. [*MGR Equipment*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1846806/mgr-equip-v-wilson-ice-enterprises/).

# Part 11 — Traps, ranked

Ordered by how often they appear as the dispositive defect in the Supreme Court corpus.

| # | Trap | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 🔴 **A joint proposal that does not apportion** — including spouses, unified claims, and claims where apportionment is illogical | Part 4. Apportion, or restructure so the proposal binds only its named offeror/offeree |
| **2** | 🔴 **Citing rule 1.442 without citing § 768.79** | Cite both, in the first sentence. [*Campbell*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140885/campbell-v-goldman/) |
| **3** | 🔴 **Assuming a motion to enlarge tolls the 30-day acceptance period** | It does not. [*Koppel*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4498881/donna-koppel-v-laura-ochoa/) |
| **4** | 🔴 **Counting extra days for service on the 30-day clock** | Rule 1.442(f)(1) excludes rule 2.514(b) |
| **5** | 🔴 **Missing the rule 1.525 deadline in reliance on a reservation of jurisdiction** | [*Saia*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1899565/saia-motor-freight-line-inc-v-reid/). Serve the fee motion early; in a jury case, calendar from the **verdict** |
| **6** | **A general release or condition stated too vaguely to price** | Attach it; state it with particularity. [*Nichols*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1697927/state-farm-mut-auto-ins-co-v-nichols/) |
| **7** | **Serving a proposal in an action seeking damages *and* equitable relief** | [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/). Check the prayer for relief first |
| **8** | **Expecting fees after a voluntary dismissal without prejudice** | [*MX Investments*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1799433/mx-investments-inc-v-crawford/) |
| **9** | **Aggregating separate proposals against one judgment** | [*Anderson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4317987/troy-anderson-v-hilton-hotels-corporation-etc/). Each proposal stands alone |
| **10** | **Conditioning a multi-offeree proposal on mutual acceptance** | [*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/), unless § 768.79(6) applies |
| **11** | **Omitting the (c)(2)(F) attorney's-fees statement where fees were pleaded** | [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/). Include it always |
| **12** | **Accepting a proposal silent as to fees while holding a separate fee entitlement** | [*Unicare*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1670587/unicare-health-facilities-inc-v-mort/). Resolve fees before accepting |
| **13** | **Ignoring foreseeable setoffs when pricing the number** | [*Rollins*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1770539/rollins-v-pizzarelli/). Model the comparator net |

## The numbering trap

🔴 **Chapter 2022-271 renumbered § 768.79. There are two different subsection (6)s.**

| Provision | Pre-2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Entitlement | (1) | (1) |
| Offer requirements | (2) | (2) |
| Property-insurer conditional joint offers | *did not exist* | **(6)** |
| Judgment comparison / the 25% math | **(6)** | **(7)** |
| Good faith | (7)(a) | **(8)(a)** |
| Reasonableness factors (amount) | (7)(b) | **(8)(b)** |

**Every pin cite must be tied to a statutory year**, and the governing version keys to the **accrual date** ([*Jones Boatyard*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1925102/metropolitan-dade-county-v-jones-boatyard-inc/)), not the date of the offer or today's book. A brief citing "§ 768.79(6)" without a year is ambiguous on its face — and if it is quoting a pre-2022 case, it almost certainly means what is now (7).

## The terminology trap (for research)

Pre-1996 opinions say "**offer of judgment**" or "**demand for judgment**." Post-1996 they say "**proposal for settlement**." Searching on the modern phrase silently truncates the case law at 1997. **Search on "768.79" and "1.442."**

# Part 12 — Unsettled ground

Where the law is genuinely open. Do not build a client strategy on any of these without disclosing the risk.

## A. Good faith is the least-developed area in the regime

Five decisions touch it and **four are asides**. No decision in the read Supreme Court corpus squarely resolves a good-faith challenge. Since good faith is the **only** discretionary escape from an otherwise mechanical entitlement, this is simultaneously the biggest gap and the biggest opportunity. The nominal-proposal question — flagged by the Court in 1992 and never answered — is the sharpest edge of it.

## B. The [*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/) reservation

Whether § 768.79 applies where monetary and nonmonetary claims are pled **separately** and the proposal is directed **only to the monetary claim**. Expressly left open in 2013 and still open. A tempting workaround; an untested one.

## C. [*Wheaton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4581389/sandra-kent-wheaton-v-mardella-wheaton/)'s alternative holding

[*Wheaton*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4581389/sandra-kent-wheaton-v-mardella-wheaton/) excused noncompliance with a **different rule set** (2.516) on a substantive-compliance rationale broader than [*Kuhajda*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4314140/susanne-l-kuhajda-v-borden-dairy-company-of-alabama-llc/)'s. [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) — decided seven years later and squarely about when strict compliance applies — **does not mention it**. Its continued vitality is unresolved.

## D. The [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula

Applied as settled law, but sustained in [*CCM*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5092491/ccm-condominium-association-inc-etc-v-petri-positive-pest-control/) on stare decisis under [*State v. Poole*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4741416/state-of-florida-v-mark-anthony-poole/) rather than on the text, over a dissent calling its supporting authorities "of very dubious provenance," and after the district court below said the plain language does not support it. **Apply it. Do not assume it is permanent.**

## E. [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) itself

🔴 Not final; rehearing and clarification pending as of August 8, 2026. Two further points:

- The **majority expressly invited a rule amendment** rather than a judicial exception, citing the 2010 adoption of (c)(4) after [*Lamb*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1950924/lamb-v-matetzschk/) as its model. The Civil Procedure Rules Committee has form here: a proposed (c)(3) carve-out was rejected in 2003, the problem it would have prevented arose in 2005, and the Court adopted essentially the original proposal in 2010. **Watch for a (c)(3) amendment proposal.**
- The Court **restyled (c)(3) unanimously on November 13, 2025** — changing only *shall* to *must*, Committee Notes unchanged — while [*Trace Elements*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10883903/trace-elements-inc-v-nadja-mackensen/) sat fully briefed on its own docket, four weeks before oral argument. Whatever else follows, the current text is the text the Court re-adopted knowing this case was coming.

## F. The Court's own assessment

Worth quoting to a client who asks why this is so hard:

> "this Court's jurisprudence **in this area of the law seems inconsistently applied and unpredictable**"
> — Polston, J., dissenting in [*Allen v. Nunez*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4540927/w-riley-allen-v-jairo-rafael-nunez/) (2018), joined by Canady, C.J., and Lawson, J.

The majority's response, in footnote 2, does not dispute the diagnosis — only what follows from it.

# Quick-reference — one-page drafting checklist

**Before drafting**

- [ ] Is this a "civil action for damages"? Any equitable claim in the case? ([*Diamond Aircraft*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4988617/diamond-aircraft-industries-inc-v-horowitch/))
- [ ] Does any contract choose another state's substantive law? ([*Southeast Floating Docks*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5056323/southeast-floating-docks-inc-v-auto-owners-insurance-co/))
- [ ] When did the cause of action accrue? Which statutory version governs? ([*Jones Boatyard*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1925102/metropolitan-dade-county-v-jones-boatyard-inc/))
- [ ] Are we inside the rule 1.442(b) window — 90 days after / 45 days before, off the **current** trial setting?
- [ ] Have I modeled the comparator under the [*White*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1912574/white-v-steak-and-ale-of-florida-inc/) formula, **net of foreseeable setoffs**?

**In the document**

- [ ] Cites **§ 768.79** by name — not just rule 1.442
- [ ] Names the offeror and the offeree; **defined terms are singular** unless a joint proposal is intended
- [ ] Nothing in the document speaks for, binds, or requires anything of a non-party to the proposal
- [ ] States that it resolves **all damages** that would otherwise be awarded in final judgment
- [ ] Every **condition** stated with particularity; any **release attached**
- [ ] **Total amount** stated; every nonmonetary term stated with particularity
- [ ] **Punitive damages** amount stated separately, if claimed
- [ ] **Attorney's-fees statement** included — whether fees are included and whether they are part of the legal claim
- [ ] **Certificate of service**
- [ ] Nothing material in the cover letter

**If joint**

- [ ] Amount **and terms** attributable to **each** party stated
- [ ] (c)(4) vicarious-liability exception applied only where liability is **solely** vicarious/constructive/derivative/technical
- [ ] If conditioned: multiple **offerors** → one offeree only. Never multiple offerees conditioned on mutual acceptance ([*Gorka*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1555417/attorneys-title-insurance-fund-inc-v-gorka/)), absent § 768.79(6)
- [ ] Allocation stress-tested against the **conjunctive 25% test** — would a weak result for one claimant sink all of them?

**On service**

- [ ] Serve, do not file (rule 1.442(d))
- [ ] Calendar **day 30** — no add-on days, no tolling by motion
- [ ] Calendar the **rule 1.525 fee-motion deadline** from the **verdict** (jury) or **entry of judgment** (nonjury)
