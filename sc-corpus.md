## Coded entries

### ✅ Willis Shaw Express, Inc. v. Hilyer Sod, Inc., 849 So. 2d 276 (Fla. 2003)

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Anstead, C.J., Pariente, Quince, Cantero, Bell, JJ., concur. **Lewis, J., concurs in result only.** Rehearing denied June 26, 2003.
- **Jurisdiction:** certified conflict, art. V, § 3(b)(4)
- **Holding:** under the plain language of rule 1.442(c)(3), an offer from multiple plaintiffs must apportion the offer among them.
- **Disposition:** approves *Hilyer Sod*, 817 So. 2d 1050 (Fla. 1st DCA 2002); **disapproves** *Flight Express v. Robinson*, 736 So. 2d 796 (Fla. 3d DCA 1999) and *Spruce Creek Dev. Co. v. Drew*, 746 So. 2d 1109 (Fla. 5th DCA 1999).
- **Canon:** derogation — and this is the **origin point** for applying it to the *rule*. The Court reasons that "the offer of judgment statute **and rule** are in derogation of the common law rule that each party pay its own fees," citing *Major League Baseball v. Morsani*, 790 So. 2d 1071 (Fla. 2001) and *Dade County v. Pena*, 664 So. 2d 959 (Fla. 1995). Both cited authorities concern **statutes**. The extension to a procedural rule is made without separate justification — precisely the move Sasso, J., attacks 23 years later in *Trace Elements*.
- **District split captured:** 1st + 2d (*Allstate v. Materiale*, 787 So. 2d 173 (Fla. 2d DCA 2001)) **vs.** 3d + 5th.
- **Rule text at the time:** "A joint proposal **shall** state the amount and terms attributable to each party" (pre-2021 restyling to "must").
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance · #2 derogation canon · #3 apportionment

### ✅ Wheaton v. Wheaton, 261 So. 3d 1236 (Fla. 2019)

- **Author:** Quince, J. — Pariente, Lewis, Polston, Labarga, JJ., concur. **Canady, C.J., concurs in result, joined by Lawson, J.** No rehearing allowed.
- **Jurisdiction:** express and direct conflict, art. V, § 3(b)(3)
- **Holding:** proposals for settlement are **not** subject to the e-mail service requirements of Fla. R. Jud. Admin. 2.516, because a proposal is neither a pleading nor a document filed in a court proceeding.
- **Alternative holding — the doctrinally significant one:** *even if* 2.516 applied, noncompliance would not invalidate the proposal, because it satisfied the substantive requirements of § 768.79. Quoting *Kuhajda*: "a procedural rule should not be strictly construed to defeat a statute it is designed to implement," and "[t]he procedural rule should no more be allowed to trump the statute here than the tail should be allowed to wag the dog."
- **Disposition:** quashes *Wheaton*, 217 So. 3d 125 (Fla. 3d DCA 2017); approves *Boatright* (2d DCA), *McCoy* (4th DCA), *Oldcastle* (1st DCA).
- **Canon:** substance-over-form / rule-serves-statute. **Directly opposed in method to *Willis Shaw*.**
- **District split captured:** 3d DCA alone **vs.** 1st + 2d + 4th.
- **Separate opinion:** Canady, C.J., would hold rule 2.516(b) *does* reach unfiled documents — reading subdivision (b) as independent of (a)'s "filed" limitation — but agrees the proposal survives. Also notes rule 1.442(c)(2)(G)'s cross-reference to rule 1.080 is an **obsolete reference to a superseded rule** that "can only be treated as meaningless."
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance (anti-strict pole) · #2 derogation canon (rejected) · service/timing

- **Holding:** rule 1.442(c)(3) requires apportionment of a joint proposal even as to a **unified, single claim**.

#### The rehearing record — motion and response read in full (Aug. 3, 2026)

Both saved in `Trace Elements/`. **Underlying facts now concrete:** county court, Indian River County, **L.T. No. 312022CC000387**; proposal **$10,000**, unapportioned, joint; jury verdict **$41,273.70**.

**Respondents' Motion for Clarification and Rehearing (7/17/2026)** — Michael Fox Orr and Loreyn P. Raab, Orr | Cook (appellate counsel newly appearing over trial counsel Kissner/Unruh).
- **Narrow by design:** "clarification on one point from the Opinion and rehearing on that lone basis."
- **The theory:** apportionment is possible under the *rule* but makes the *statute* unenforceable. Splits § 768.79 into the proposal (subsections (1)–(6)) and the application of the proposal to the judgment (**subsection (7)**), and argues nothing tells a trial court how to run the 25% comparison against an apportioned proposal for a unified claim.
- **Four options walk-through** at 4–6: unapportioned $10,000 (what they served); 50/50; 90/10; and 50/50 with a fight over verdict-form lines. In each apportioned variant, offeror and offeree can each argue a different comparator off a single-line verdict.
- ⭐ **The constitutional turn** at 7–8: strict compliance with (c)(3) could **enlarge** the substantive right, because splitting the offer lowers each offeror's number and makes the 25% threshold easier to beat. Cites ***Ramos v. State***, 505 So. 2d 418, 421 (Fla. 1987) (quoting *State v. Furen*, 118 So. 2d 6 (Fla. 1960)): "substantive rights conferred by law can neither be diminished nor enlarged by procedural rules adopted by this Court." Frames it as the Legislature meaning to promote settlement, "not create a **sword** whereby a married couple could engineer a claim for fees where previously the law provided none."
  - ⭐⭐ **This is the *Knealing* / art. V, § 2(a) axis pointed in the unfamiliar direction** — a substance/procedure attack arguing the rule **expands** rather than contracts the statutory right. Fits the existing warning that strict construction is not inherently pro-offeree.
- Also cites ***Beal Bank, SSB v. Almand & Assocs.***, 780 So. 2d 45, 53 (Fla. 2001) — no substantive basis to sever a unified entireties claim.
- Concedes the opinion "suggests a rule change may be appropriate," and concedes counsel "was unable to find any way to apportion an offer … which could give credit to section 768.79(7)."

**Petitioner's Response in Opposition (7/20/2026)** — Andrew A. Harris, Harris Appeals.
- **Three-part answer:** (1) no ambiguity, so nothing to clarify (*Whipple v. State*, 431 So. 2d 1011, 1013 (Fla. 2d DCA 1983); *Jedak Corp. v. Seabreeze Off. Assocs.*, 248 So. 3d 242, 244 n.1 (Fla. 5th DCA 2018)); (2) it is a request for an **advisory opinion** on hypothetical proposals never served (*Fernandez v. Gonzalez*, 405 So. 3d 531, 532 n.1 (Fla. 3d DCA 2025); *Nguyen v. Williams*, 429 So. 3d 1105, 1109 (Fla. 2d DCA 2026)); (3) the § 768.79(7) theory is **unpreserved** — never raised in the trial court, the Fourth District, or the answer brief — and rule **9.330(a)(2)(A)** bars new issues on rehearing (*Sanchez v. Miami-Dade County*, 286 So. 3d 191, 195 (Fla. 2019) — "changed horses in midstream," "[t]hat doesn't work"; *Ellison v. Willoughby*; *Citizens v. Clark*; *Dabbs v. State*).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The response's Section VI answers the administrability objection — and in doing so supplies a second, non-*Fabre* purpose for (c)(3).** It cites ***Hoang Dinh Duong v. Ziadie***, 153 So. 3d 354, 358–60 (Fla. 4th DCA 2014), which applies the 25% comparison **to each joint offeror's allocated amount** and says allocation serves "**the other main purpose of the apportionment requirement**" — *enabling the fee determination*. On the facts here: half of $41,273.70 = $20,636.85, which beats each spouse's hypothetical $5,000 share by far more than 25%.
  - ✅ **RESOLVED August 3, 2026 — the counter fails.** Full analysis in `CLAUDE.md`. Short version: the administrability rationale appears in this Court **exactly once**, in *Hingson* at 199, as the sentence immediately preceding "This, we believe" — the same contested 4–3 passage the line already rests on, and *about the statute, not (c)(3)*. **Zero** occurrences of "twenty-five percent" in *Willis Shaw*, *Gorka*, *Pratt*, *Audiffred*, or *Trace Elements*; *Lamb* repeats it only by block quote; *Trace Elements* cites *Hingson* 199 only for the independent-evaluation half. **Harding's dissent already answered it**, and *Hingson*'s own n.3 example works only because the Herzogs had **two different judgments** ($8,601 / $3,750). Thesis point 2 stands and gains a limb.
- ⭐ **fn.3 — the practitioner workaround, and an arc-8 problem.** Joint apportioned offers may include "simple language making it clear the recipient must accept the entire, allocated offer" — the "**all-or-nothing, allocated offer**," which "all of the district courts of appeal to have reviewed the issue have approved," citing *Hoang Dinh Duong*, 153 So. 3d at 358–60 (collecting cases). ⚠️ **Reconcile with *Gorka***, which invalidated a joint proposal requiring joint acceptance, and with the ch. 2022-271 override. Straight to the practitioner checklist either way.
- **Section V — the factual attack that may matter most.** This may never have been a unified-claim case: the Mackensens pleaded breach of contract **and unjust enrichment**, and Trace Elements counterclaimed against **each Mackensen individually** for tortious interference, attaching emails from **Mr. Mackensen only**. The directed verdict on tortious interference came **eight months after** the proposal was served, and a proposal is judged **as of the time of service**. So the "unified, single claim" premise is contested on the record.
- Forecloses the workability objection with ***Pratt***: "Even where no logical apportionment can be made, it is nonetheless required," and practical complications "are not recognized legal elements to disregard or circumvent the rule," 161 So. 3d at 1272–73; plus *Lamb*'s "it may take some creative drafting."
- Points to the **rulemaking process** as the remedy, citing the post-*Lamb* adoption of (c)(4) in 52 So. 3d 579 (Fla. 2010) — the same move the majority made.

#### ⭐ Assessment: denial is the likely outcome

Not a prediction to put in a deliverable, but it should drive sequencing. The motion is narrow, concedes the holding, and rests on a theory the response documents as raised for the first time on rehearing — which rule 9.330(a)(2)(A) forecloses outright. The response also lands a straightforward arithmetic answer to the hypotheticals and a record-based challenge to the unified-claim premise. **Plan on the opinion standing.** But do not write as if it has: a *clarification* grant addressing the § 768.79(7) mechanics is the live tail risk, and it would touch the administrability question above.

### ✅ TGI Friday's, Inc. v. Dvorak, 663 So. 2d 606 (Fla. 1995)

- **Author:** Overton, J. — Grimes, C.J., Kogan, Harding, JJ., concur. Shaw, J., concurs in result only. **Wells, J., concurs in part and dissents in part.** Anstead, J., recused. Rehearing denied Nov. 27, 1995.
- **Structural finding — the three-instrument problem.** Before 1996, offers of judgment were governed *simultaneously* by **§ 45.061**, **§ 768.79**, and **rule 1.442 (1990)**, "all of which employ different language." Dvorak served three separate offers, one under each. Any pre-1996 analysis that treats § 768.79 as the sole regime is wrong.
- **Constitutionality:** § 768.79 upheld. The Legislature may create a substantive right to fees; the *procedural* portions of the statute were superseded by rule 1.442 under art. V, § 2(a). Follows *Leapai v. Milton*, 595 So. 2d 12 (Fla. 1992) (upholding § 45.061).
- **Holding:** § 768.79 entitlement does **not** require that the rejection be unreasonable. Adopts the entitlement/award framework of *Schmidt v. Fortner*, 629 So. 2d 1036 (Fla. 4th DCA 1993): entitlement is a pure arithmetic calculation; the only escape is a § 768.79(7)(a) finding that the offer was not made in good faith; the (7)(b) factors go to the *amount*, not entitlement.
- **Disposition:** approves *Dvorak*, 639 So. 2d 58 (Fla. 4th DCA 1994) and *Schmidt*; **disapproves** *Bridges v. Newton*, 556 So. 2d 1170 (Fla. 3d DCA 1990) to the extent it required unreasonable rejection.
- **Retroactivity:** rule 1.442 may apply where the *rejection* post-dates the rule's effective date, even if the cause of action pre-dates it (following *Leapai*).
- ⭐ **Genealogy of the strict-construction canon.** Wells, J., writing separately, is the **first member of this Court to argue § 768.79 must be strictly construed** — both as a fee statute in derogation of the American Rule (citing *Gershuny*) and as "a sanction"/"in the nature of a penalty." Wells then **authors *Willis Shaw* in 2003**, converting the 1995 separate opinion into the majority rule. Later cases cite *TGI Friday's* at **615** — a page inside Wells's separate opinion — as authority for strict construction (see *Diamond Aircraft*, below). **This is the single cleanest doctrinal thread found so far.**
- Confirms ch. 90-119 "substantially amended" the statute and added subsection (6).
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance (origin) · #2 derogation canon (origin) · #6 good faith · #7 retroactivity

### ✅ Rollins v. Pizzarelli, 761 So. 2d 294 (Fla. 2000) — ⚠️ peripheral

- **Author:** Per curiam **on rehearing** (the Feb. 4, 1999 opinion was withdrawn and replaced — Harding, C.J., dissenting, calls it a "judicial U-turn"). Anstead, Pariente, Lewis, Quince concur. Harding, C.J., dissents with Wells, J.; Wells, J., dissents separately; Shaw, J., dissents. **4–3.**
- **Actual holding:** construes "paid or payable" in § 627.736(3) (PIP setoff) to exclude future unincurred medical expenses. **Not a § 768.79 case.**
- **The § 768.79 connection is purely consequential:** the PIP setoff had reduced the judgment from $25,048 to $24,523.22, dropping it below the 25% threshold and thereby destroying fee entitlement on a $20,000 demand.
- ⚠️ **Methodological warning.** Its 121 citations are almost entirely for the PIP holding. **Global `citeCount` is a poor proxy for doctrinal relevance in this corpus.** Rank by citations *within* the 768.79/1.442 corpus, not globally — otherwise the priority queue fills with insurance cases that merely touch the statute.
- **Arc:** none (calculation of "judgment obtained" only)

### ✅ State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Nichols, 932 So. 2d 1067 (Fla. 2006)

- **Author:** Cantero, J. — Pariente, C.J., Wells, Lewis, Bell, JJ., concur. **Anstead, J., concurs in result only, joined by Quince, J.** 5–2 on reasoning.
- **Holding 1 — scope.** A PIP suit is a "civil action for damages" under § 768.79(1). Plain meaning; "[n]othing in the offer of judgment statute exempts claims for contractual damages." Section 627.428's one-way fee street does **not** preclude § 768.79 — the two operate on different periods (pre-offer fees to the insured, post-offer fees to the insurer), illustrated by a four-row chart in the opinion. Rejects the access-to-courts challenge (*Kluger*, *Lasky*, *Chapman*, *Rowe*).
- **Holding 2 — the ambiguity standard.** A general release is a "condition" or "nonmonetary term" that must be stated with particularity under rule 1.442(c)(2)(C)–(D). **The operative test:** a summary suffices "as long as it eliminates any reasonable ambiguity about its scope"; the rule "does not demand the impossible," only that the proposal be "sufficiently clear and definite to allow the offeree to make an informed decision without needing clarification." **"If ambiguity within the proposal could reasonably affect the offeree's decision, the proposal will not satisfy the particularity requirement."** And: **"The rule aims to prevent ambiguity, not breadth"** — expansive boilerplate releases are fine if unambiguous.
- **Resolves a district split** on whether releases are conditions at all: majority view (1st, 2d, 4th, 5th) vs. the Third District's *Earnest & Stewart, Inc. v. Codina*, 732 So. 2d 364 (Fla. 3d DCA 1999) line treating releases as "mere surplusage." Court adopts the majority view.
- Quotes *Lucas v. Calhoun*, 813 So. 2d 971 (Fla. 2d DCA 2002): "Proposals for settlement are intended to end judicial labor, not create more."
- ⭐ **A second override call.** Anstead, J., concurring in result, writes that the majority has "eviscerated" the Legislature's intent to limit PIP fees to insureds and that "one can only hope that the Legislature will recognize that its work has been undone, and act promptly to restore the balance." **The Legislature eventually acted — but in the opposite direction**, repealing § 627.428 outright in 2023 (HB 837). Worth developing: the dialogue runs both ways, and legislative responses have not consistently favored claimants.
- **Arc:** #4 ambiguity/nonmonetary terms (the governing standard) · #5 scope

### ✅ Diamond Aircraft Industries, Inc. v. Horowitch, 107 So. 3d 362 (Fla. 2013)

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Pariente, Quince, Labarga, Perry, JJ., concur. Polston, C.J., concurs in result. **Canady, J., concurs in part and dissents in part.**
- **Posture:** four questions certified by the Eleventh Circuit (two on FDUTPA fees under § 501.2105, two on § 768.79).
- **Holding 1 — scope.** § 768.79 does **not** apply to an action seeking both damages and equitable relief where the offer is a *general* one resolving all claims. **No exception** for equitable claims that lack serious merit. Two grounds: (a) strict construction of "any civil action for damages"; (b) a structural argument — the statute supplies no method to value nonmonetary relief against an offer, since § 768.79(6) works by arithmetic comparison of "judgment obtained" to "amount of the offer."
  - Pleading in the alternative doesn't help: "an equitable claim in the alternative to a monetary claim is still part of the same civil action."
  - Approves the reasoning of *Palm Beach Polo Holdings*, 22 So. 3d 140 (Fla. 4th DCA 2009) and *Winter Park Imports*, 66 So. 3d 336 (Fla. 5th DCA 2011).
  - ⭐ **Expressly leaves open** whether § 768.79 applies where monetary and nonmonetary claims are pled separately and the offer is **directed specifically to the monetary claim**. A live, undecided gap — and a practitioner workaround worth flagging in the checklist.
- **Holding 2 — particularity.** An offer that fails to state whether it includes attorney's fees and whether fees are part of the legal claim (rule 1.442(c)(2)(F)) is invalid **where the complaint actually pleaded a fee claim**. Draws "into question" *Bennett v. American Learning Systems*, 857 So. 2d 986 (Fla. 4th DCA 2003).
- **Canon:** derogation **and** penal. Cites *Campbell v. Goldman*, 959 So. 2d 223, 226–27 (Fla. 2007) ("all portions must be strictly construed") and **_TGI Friday's_, 663 So. 2d at 615** — a page inside **Wells, J.'s separate opinion**, confirming the genealogy above.
- **Related:** *Southeast Floating Docks*, 82 So. 3d 73 (Fla. 2012) held § 768.79 substantive for **both constitutional and conflict-of-law purposes**, so it has no application where the parties contracted for another state's substantive law. Distinguished here because the choice-of-law provision selected *Florida* law and only one of four claims was governed by Arizona law.
- ⭐ **Tension to resolve:** *Diamond Aircraft* (2013) invalidates under **(c)(2)(F)**; *Kuhajda* (2016) **excuses** noncompliance with the very same subdivision. The cases are reconcilable on their facts — fees pleaded here, not pleaded in *Kuhajda* — which means *Kuhajda* effectively **revived *Bennett*** and confined *Diamond Aircraft*. *Trace Elements* then distinguishes *Kuhajda* as a (c)(2)(F) case. **Verify this reading against *Kuhajda* and *Campbell* directly before relying on it.**
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance · #2 derogation canon · #4 particularity · #5 scope

### ✅ Campbell v. Goldman, 959 So. 2d 223 (Fla. 2007)

- **Author:** Quince, J. — Lewis, C.J., Wells, Cantero, JJ., concur. **Pariente, J., specially concurring, joined by Anstead, J. Bell, J., concurring in result only, with opinion.**
- **Holding:** a proposal must cite § 768.79 itself; citing only rule 1.442 is fatal. § 768.79(2)(a) requires the offer "state that it is being made pursuant to this section," and rule 1.442(c)(1) requires it "identify the applicable Florida law."
- **Disposition:** quashes *Goldman v. Campbell*, 920 So. 2d 1264 (Fla. 4th DCA 2006); approves *McMullen Oil* (2d DCA 1997) and *Pippin* (1st DCA 1993); rejects the *Spruce Creek* (5th DCA 1999) "insignificant technical violation" approach.
- **The canonical strict-construction formulation** — this is the sentence everything later cites: "**strict construction is applicable to both the substantive and procedural portions of the rule and statute** … This is a mandatory requirement for this penal, fee-shifting provision. Because the overall subject is in derogation of the common law, **all portions must be strictly construed**."
- ⭐ **The derogation canon is attacked from inside the Court in 2007 — not first in 2016 or 2026.** Bell, J., concurring in result only: "I do not believe it is appropriate to invoke the **questionable derogation canon**." His three moves are the exact moves Sasso, J., makes in *Trace Elements* nineteen years later:
  1. The statute and rule are unambiguous, so no canon is needed at all (*A.R. Douglass*).
  2. "[I]f this court rule was ambiguous, the standard of construction stated in **rule 1.010** would apply, not the derogation canon."
  3. Quoting Farmer, J., below: "the derogation canon — created for **statutory** changes in substantive common law — has no logical purpose or use in the interpretation of mere rules of procedure."
  - Bell also cites **Fordham & Leach, *Interpretation of Statutes in Derogation of the Common Law*, 3 Vand. L. Rev. 438 (1950)**.
  - ***Trace Elements* (2026) cites this very opinion** — *Campbell*, 959 So. 2d at 228 (Bell, J., concurring in result only) — for the rule 1.010 point. **A second dissent-to-doctrine thread, running opposite the Wells thread.**
- **Pariente, J., specially concurring** — "I *reluctantly* agree." Notes that after *Sarkis*, "[t]here is now only one statute governing offers of judgments implemented by rule 1.442," so the citation requirement "no longer has any true meaning." Source of the much-quoted line that parties are on notice "all 't's' must be crossed and 'i's' dotted" (later thrown back at the Court in *Wheaton*). Doubts whether the regime encourages settlement "or at times is having the opposite effect of increasing litigation."
- ✅ **Resolved by *CCM Condominium* (2021):** Pariente's "only one statute" remark was practical, not literal. **§ 45.061 was never repealed** — it survives as § 45.061, Fla. Stat. (2020), reaching only causes of action that accrued on or before its 1990 effective date. Vestigial, but still on the books, and its § 45.061(2)(b) definition of "the amount of the judgment" is the comparator Canady deploys against the *White* formula.
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance · #2 derogation canon (first internal challenge)

### ✅ Kuhajda v. Borden Dairy Co. of Alabama, LLC, 202 So. 3d 391 (Fla. 2016)

- **Author:** Canady, J. — Labarga, C.J., Pariente, Lewis, Quince, Perry, JJ., concur. Polston, J., concurs in result. **Unanimous in result.**
- **Holding:** an offer is **not** invalid for failing to state whether it includes attorney's fees and whether fees are part of the legal claim (rule 1.442(c)(2)(F)) **if fees are not sought in the pleadings**.
- **Disposition:** quashes *Borden Dairy*, 171 So. 3d 242 (Fla. 1st DCA 2015); **approves *Bennett***, 857 So. 2d 986 (Fla. 4th DCA 2003); **disapproves *Colvin v. Clements & Ashmore*, 182 So. 3d 924 (Fla. 1st DCA 2016)**.
- ✅ **Confirms the *Diamond Aircraft* tension flagged earlier — and resolves it exactly as predicted.** The Court distinguishes *Diamond Aircraft* on the pleaded-fees ground and expressly walks back its own dictum: "Although we acknowledge that this statement might be understood as questioning the **holding** of *Bennett*, we believe that it is instead properly understood in context as an observation regarding the **dicta** in *Bennett*." So *Kuhajda* revives *Bennett* and confines *Diamond Aircraft* to cases where fees were actually pleaded.
- ⚠️ **CORRECTION to this project's earlier framing.** *Kuhajda* does **not** reject strict construction or the derogation canon. It carves a **narrow exception** turning on a substantive/procedural line, and it says so precisely:
  > "This Court required a strict construction of rule 1.442 … in *Audiffred*, *Pratt*, *Gorka*, *Campbell*, *Lamb*, and *Willis Shaw* **in contexts in which the provisions of the rule implemented the substantive requirements of section 768.79**. Notably, **none of those cases explicitly addresses whether a provision of rule 1.442 must be strictly enforced when the provision is a requirement that does not implement a substantive requirement** of section 768.79."
  - "The only purpose of rule 1.442 is to provide a procedural framework to implement the substantive requirements of section 768.79."
  - "Rule 1.442(c)(2)(F)'s requirements relating to attorney's fees are **totally irrelevant** to the settlement of a case in which attorney's fees are not sought."
  - "The procedural rule should no more be allowed to trump the statute here than the tail should be allowed to wag the dog. A procedural rule should not be strictly construed to defeat a statute it is designed to implement."
- **Ties the test to art. V, § 2(a):** when the Court adopted the 1996 amendments it explained those provisions were "procedural" and therefore within its province, citing 682 So. 2d 105, 106 (Fla. 1996).
- Cites *Stockman v. Downs*, 573 So. 2d 835 (Fla. 1991) — a fee claim must be pleaded.
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance (the carve-out) · #2 derogation canon · #4 particularity

### ✅ Allstate Indemnity Co. v. Hingson, 808 So. 2d 197 (Fla. 2002) — ⭐ the foundation stone

- **Author:** Per curiam — Wells, C.J., Pariente, Lewis, Quince, JJ. **Harding, J., dissents, joined by Shaw and Anstead, JJ. 4–3.**
- **Critical posture, easy to miss:** this was a **pre-1997 offer**, governed by the *former* rule 1.442, which read in its entirety: "Parties shall comply with the procedure set forth in section 768.79, Florida Statutes." **The apportionment language of current (c)(3) did not yet exist.** The Court therefore had to derive apportionment from **the statute alone**.
- **The entire textual basis is two sentences:**
  > "Moreover, the plain language of section 768.79 supports the *C & S* court's holding. **In subsection (2)(b), the statute refers to 'party' in the singular. This, we believe, indicates the Legislature's intent that an offer specify the amount attributable to each individual party.**"
  - Note the hedge — "we believe."
- **Plus a functional argument — the administrability rationale, now quoted exactly** (it is the sentence *immediately preceding* the two above):
  > "We agree with the district court in *C & S* that '[t]o further the statute's goal, each party who receive[s] an offer of settlement is entitled … to evaluate the offer as it pertains to him or her.' **Otherwise, in many cases, it would be impossible for the trial court to determine the amount attributable to each party in order to make a further determination of whether the judgment against only one of the parties was at least twenty-five percent more or less than the offer** (depending on which party made the offer)."
  - ⭐⭐ **This single sentence is the entire Supreme Court source for the fee-administrability justification.** *Hoang Dinh Duong v. Ziadie*, 153 So. 3d 354, 358–60 (Fla. 4th DCA 2014), cites it for "the other main purpose of the apportionment requirement." **Verified August 3, 2026: it recurs nowhere else in this Court.** "Twenty-five percent" appears **zero** times in *Willis Shaw*, *Gorka*, *Pratt*, *Audiffred*, and *Trace Elements*; *Lamb* reproduces it only inside a block quote of *Hingson*; *Trace Elements* cites *Hingson* at 199 **only** for the independent-evaluation half.
  - Note the hedge here too — "**in many cases**."
  - ⚠️ Both halves of the passage — independent evaluation *and* administrability — are **quoted from *C & S Chemicals*, a district court decision.** This Court's contribution is the singular-"party" inference and the word "Otherwise."
- ⭐⭐ **Footnote 3 undercuts the rationale it supports.** The worked example is *Herzog*: K-Mart offered the Herzogs **$20,001** jointly; judgment came back **$8,601 for Mrs. Herzog and $3,750 for Mr. Herzog**, and only then is it "not necessarily clear" whether the 25% test was met. **The illustration works only because the spouses had two different judgments.** In a unified-claim case there is one judgment and the arithmetic is trivial — which is exactly what petitioner's own *Trace Elements* rehearing response demonstrates. **The Court's only concrete example of why apportionment is needed is a separable-claims case.**
- **Disposition:** approves *Hingson* (2d DCA) and *C & S Chemicals* (2d DCA 2000); **disapproves _Herzog v. K-Mart_**, 760 So. 2d 1006 (Fla. 4th DCA 2000).
- ⭐ **Harding's dissent refutes the singular-"party" inference with a statute.** **§ 1.01(1), Fla. Stat.**: "In construing these statutes and each and every word, phrase, or part hereof, where the context will permit … [t]he singular includes the plural and vice versa." The Legislature's own general definitions provision directly contradicts the majority's only textual ground.
- Harding also records the alignment: "**Of the four district courts that have considered this issue, only the Second District has held to the contrary.**" The Court adopted a **1-of-4 minority position**.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Harding answered the administrability rationale directly, and the facts of the case refute it.** Quoting the majority's "in many cases" sentence, he replies: "**Rather than worry about what may happen 'in many cases,' it is more appropriate to focus on the facts of this case.** The jury returned a verdict in favor of the Defendant, resulting in the Plaintiffs receiving nothing. **There is no question that the judgment was at least twenty-five percent less than the amount of Allstate's offer, regardless of its nonallocation.**" **In the very case that generated the administrability rationale, administrability was not a problem.** Lead with this whenever the rationale is raised.
- **Arc:** #3 apportionment (origin) · #1 strict compliance

### ✅ Attorneys' Title Insurance Fund, Inc. v. Gorka, 36 So. 3d 646 (Fla. 2010)

- **Author:** Per curiam — Pariente, Lewis, Labarga, Perry, JJ. **Polston, J., dissents, joined by Quince, C.J., and Canady, J. 4–3.**
- **Holding:** a joint proposal **conditioned on mutual acceptance** by all joint offerees is invalid and unenforceable, because neither offeree can independently evaluate or settle his or her own claim. "An offer that cannot be unilaterally accepted to create a binding settlement is an **illusory offer**."
- **Disposition:** approves *Attorneys' Title*, 989 So. 2d 1210 (Fla. 2d DCA 2008); **disapproves _Clements v. Rose_**, 982 So. 2d 731 (Fla. 1st DCA 2008).
- ⚠️ **The independent-acceptance requirement appears nowhere in the statute or the rule.** The Court derives it from precedent, and says so: from (c)(3)'s apportionment requirement and (c)(2)(C)'s conditions requirement, "[a] review of our precedent reveals that this principle **inherently requires** that an offer … be structured such that either offeree can independently evaluate and settle his or her respective claim." Built on *Hingson* → *Willis Shaw* → *Lamb*.
- Expressly restates *Hingson*'s singular-"party" reading as the statutory anchor.
- ⭐ **The Court concedes the regime is counterproductive:** "The expected result of the attorneys' fee sanction was to reduce litigation costs and conserve judicial resources … **The effect, however, has been in sharp contrast to the intended outcome because the statute and rule have seemingly increased litigation** as parties dispute the respective validity and enforceability of these offers," quoting *Security Professionals, Inc. v. Segall*, 685 So. 2d 1381, 1384 (Fla. 4th DCA 1997). **Quotable admission against interest — put it in the memo.**
- Sharp exchange with the dissent: the majority calls the dissent's reading a "phantom offer" that "would never produce a settlement"; the dissent (per *Trace Elements*' quotation of it) says the majority "effectively eliminates the ability to make joint offers."
- ⭐ **Overridden by the Legislature** — ch. 2022-271 added § 768.79(6) permitting joint settlements conditioned on mutual acceptance in a specified circumstance. The Court then conformed the rule, 345 So. 3d 845 (Fla. 2022).
- ⚠️ **Reclassified after reading the dissent:** the proposal here **was** fully apportioned ($12,500 to each plaintiff). *Gorka* is a **conditions** case, not an apportionment case. See the dissent section below.
- **Arc:** #4 conditions/(c)(2)(C) · #1 strict compliance · the override-and-conform spine

### ✅ Lamb v. Matetzschk, 906 So. 2d 1037 (Fla. 2005) — ⭐ the most useful case in the corpus

- **Author:** Quince, J. — Wells, Cantero, Bell, JJ., concur (**four**). **Pariente, C.J., specially concurring, joined by Anstead and Lewis, JJ. Lewis, J., concurring in result only, with opinion.** Unanimous in result, but **three justices write or join to say the rule is broken**.
- **Holding:** rule 1.442(c)(3) requires differentiated offers even where a party's liability is **purely vicarious**. Approves *Matetzschk* (5th DCA); **disapproves _Barnes v. Kellogg_** (2d DCA 2003).
- Source of the line Sasso, J., later cites as evidence of impracticality: "It may take some **creative drafting** to fashion an offer of settlement when one party is only vicariously liable."
- ⭐⭐ **The *Fabre* origin of (c)(3) — this reframes the entire apportionment line.** Per Pariente, C.J.: "**The committee notes to rule 1.442 state that this requirement was added to conform the rule to our decision in _Fabre v. Marin_, 623 So. 2d 1182 (Fla. 1993)**, in which we interpreted a statute requiring apportionment of liability for **noneconomic damages**." So (c)(3) was designed to track **apportionment of fault among tortfeasors**, not to police who signs an offer.
  - Pariente then states the consequence directly: "**Our recent interpretation of the plain language of rule 1.442(c)(3) has broadened the reach of the rule beyond _Fabre_ so that a joint offer to a husband and wife is no longer authorized**," citing *Hingson*. And: "by requiring apportionment of an offer of judgment directed to a husband and wife, the rule imposes a restriction that does not comport with the manner in which most settlements are accomplished."
  - ⚠️ ***Trace Elements* (2026) is a husband-and-wife case.** Pariente identified that exact application as beyond the rule's purpose **twenty-one years earlier**.
- ⭐⭐ **The 2003 rejected amendment — a documented seven-year loop.** The Civil Procedure Rules Committee proposed amending (c)(3) to excuse apportionment for parties "alleged to be vicariously, constructively, derivatively or technically liable." **The Court declined**, expressly "in light of our decision in *Willis Shaw*." *Amendments to the Fla. Rules of Civil Procedure*, 858 So. 2d 1013, 1014–15 (Fla. 2003). Two years later *Lamb* created precisely the problem the committee had tried to prevent. Five years after that the Court adopted essentially the committee's proposal as **(c)(4)**, 52 So. 3d 579, 588 (Fla. 2010), effective 2011.
  - **Committee proposes fix → Court refuses → Court creates the problem → Court adopts the fix.** Put this in the memo.
- ⭐ **Third dissent-to-doctrine thread.** Lewis, J., concurring in result only, is "compelled … solely and exclusively upon the plain language," calls apportionment between an active and a vicariously liable tortfeasor "meaningless and essentially unworkable" (a vicariously liable party gets total indemnity, *Houdaille Indus. v. Edwards*, 374 So. 2d 490 (Fla. 1979)), and rejects the majority's reasoning outright: *Willis Shaw*, *Hingson*, and *Barnes* are "**entirely distinguishable**," and "**[t]he result, in my view, is purely the product of the technical language of the rule, not logic or proper legal reasoning.**"
  - **This is Sasso, J.'s argument in *Trace Elements*, made in 2005.**
- **Arc:** #3 apportionment · #1 strict compliance · rulemaking dialogue

### ✅ Pratt v. Weiss, 161 So. 3d 1268 (Fla. 2015)

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Labarga, C.J., Pariente, Quince, Perry, JJ., concur. **Canady, J., dissents, joined by Polston, J. 5–2.**
- **Holding:** a proposal titled as coming from one entity but whose text refers to the offerors **in the plural** is a joint proposal requiring apportionment. A limited partnership and its general partner are distinct entities (§ 620.1104(1), Fla. Stat.). Quashes *Pratt* (4th DCA).
- **The high-water mark of formalism:** "**Even where no logical apportionment can be made, it is nonetheless required where more than one offeror or offeree is involved**" (citing *Easters v. Russell*, 942 So. 2d 1008 (Fla. 2d DCA 2006)). Difficulty of apportionment, coextensive liability, and the ability to apportion *after* acceptance are all "not recognized legal elements to disregard or circumvent the rule."
- Records the Fourth District's characterization of apportionment as a "**bright line rule**, to be applied without exception" (*Cano*, *Graham*).
- **Arc:** #3 apportionment · #1 strict compliance

### ✅ Audiffred v. Arnold, 161 So. 3d 1274 (Fla. 2015)

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Labarga, C.J., Pariente, Quince, Perry, JJ., concur. **Canady, J., dissents, joined by Polston, J. 5–2.** Companion to *Pratt*, same day.
- **Holding:** where a **single** offeror serves a **single** offeree but the offer would resolve claims by or against additional parties who are neither offerors nor offerees, it is a **joint proposal** subject to (c)(3). Approves *Arnold* (1st DCA); **disapproves _Andrews v. Frey_ (5th DCA), _Eastern Atlantic Realty_ (3d DCA), and _Alioto-Alexander_ (4th DCA)**.
- Facts: wife's injury claim plus husband's loss-of-consortium claim; unapportioned $17,500. Held fatally ambiguous.
- Note the **dual footing** — the Court rests on (c)(3) apportionment *and* on (c)(2)(C) particularity/ambiguity per *Nichols*. That matters: *Trace Elements* concedes its proposal was **not** ambiguous, so the *Audiffred* ambiguity rationale does not carry over.
- ⚠️ **Same 1-versus-3 district pattern as *Hingson*:** the Court again sides with a single district against three.
- **Arc:** #3 apportionment · #4 ambiguity

## ⭐ The core finding — the apportionment line rests on one contested inference, and has been repudiated at every opportunity

Reading *Hingson* and *Gorka* answers the revised central question, and the answer is sharper than expected.

### The chain, and where it comes from

| Year | Case | Vote | What it added | What happened to it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1993 | *Fabre v. Marin* | — | Apportionment of **fault** for noneconomic damages | (c)(3) added in 1996 to **conform the rule to *Fabre*** — per the committee notes |
| 2002 | *Hingson* | **4–3** | Singular "party" in § 768.79(2)(b) ⇒ apportionment required | Foundation of everything after |
| 2003 | *Willis Shaw* | 6–1 | Strict construction of rule (c)(3); derogation canon extended to the rule | Court **rejects** the Rules Committee's proposed vicarious-liability carve-out the same year, "in light of *Willis Shaw*" |
| 2005 | *Lamb* | unanimous in result, **3 justices object in writing** | Apportionment required even for purely vicarious liability | **Overridden by the Court's own rule amendment**, 52 So. 3d 579, 588 (Fla. 2010), adopting (c)(4) — essentially the 2003 proposal it had refused |
| 2010 | *Gorka* | **4–3** | Independent-acceptance requirement, derived "inherently" from precedent — **note: that offer *was* fully apportioned**, so this is a conditions case, not an apportionment case | **Overridden by the Legislature**, ch. 2022-271 § 768.79(6), adopting Polston's dissent; rule conformed, 345 So. 3d 845 (Fla. 2022) |
| 2015 | *Pratt* / *Audiffred* | **5–2** (Canady, Polston dissenting) | "Even where no logical apportionment can be made, it is nonetheless required" | Canady authors *Kuhajda* the next year |
| 2026 | *Trace Elements* | **4–3** | Apportionment required even for a unified, single claim | Rehearing period open |

### Three observations the memo should lead with

**1. The textual foundation is a single contested inference.** Everything traces to two hedged sentences in *Hingson* reading the singular "party" in § 768.79(2)(b). Made **4–3**, over a dissent citing **§ 1.01(1), Fla. Stat.** — the Legislature's own directive that "[t]he singular includes the plural and vice versa" — and against **three of the four** district courts then to have ruled. Never revisited. *Audiffred* (2015) repeats the 1-versus-3 pattern.

**2. The rule provision was built for a different job.** Per the committee notes quoted in *Lamb*, (c)(3) was added to conform rule 1.442 to ***Fabre v. Marin*** — apportionment of **fault** for noneconomic damages. Pariente, C.J., wrote in 2005 that the Court's reading "has broadened the reach of the rule **beyond *Fabre*** so that **a joint offer to a husband and wife is no longer authorized**." ***Trace Elements* is a husband-and-wife case.** The objection is twenty-one years old and came from the Chief Justice.

**3. Every extension has been repudiated by whoever got the next word — and the Court has already run this loop once.** In **2003** the Rules Committee proposed excusing apportionment for vicariously liable parties; the Court **refused**, citing *Willis Shaw*. In **2005** *Lamb* created exactly that problem, over three justices' written objections. In **2010** the Court adopted essentially the committee's original proposal as (c)(4). *Gorka* was then overridden by the **Legislature** in 2022. *Trace Elements* now invites the same treatment, expressly citing the post-*Lamb* amendment as its model.

### ✅ MGR Equipment Corp. v. Wilson Ice Enterprises, Inc., 731 So. 2d 1262 (Fla. 1999)

- **Author:** Shaw, J. — Harding, C.J., Wells, Lewis, Quince, JJ., concur. **Pariente, J., specially concurring, joined by Anstead, J.**
- **Holding:** an offer that says nothing about a pending **counterclaim** is not void. § 768.79(2) provides the "offer shall be construed as including **all damages which may be awarded in a final judgment**," so an **unqualified** offer disposes of every pending claim, counterclaims included. Approves *MGR* (5th DCA); conflict with *Hartford Casualty v. Silverman* (3d DCA 1997).
- Rests on *Unicare*'s purpose language and the "organic right … to contract a settlement," and notes § 768.79(1) calls the awards "**penalties**."
- ⭐ **Footnote 2 is the source of a much-quoted line.** Of the 1996 rule: "the current rule **mandates greater detail in settlement proposals, which will hopefully enable parties to focus with greater specificity in their negotiations and thereby facilitate more settlements and less litigation**." *Willis Shaw* quotes this in 2003 to justify strict construction, and Pariente quotes it back in her *Lamb* concurrence to show the hope was not realized. **Cite it from here.**
- **Pariente, J., specially concurring:** distinguishes *Hartford* as a **qualified** offer — the release there ran only to the plaintiff's claims, not all claims between the parties. Hopes the 1996 amendments "will eliminate the problems presented by this case."
- **Arc:** #4 conditions/scope of an offer

### ✅ White v. Steak and Ale of Florida, Inc., 816 So. 2d 546 (Fla. 2002) — ⭐ source of the *White* formula

- **Author:** Pariente, J. — Wells, C.J., Shaw, Anstead, Lewis, Quince, JJ., concur. **Harding, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part.**
- **Holding:** "judgment obtained" under § 768.79 is **not** limited to the verdict. It includes the net judgment for damages **plus** attorney's fees and taxable costs that could have been included in a final judgment **entered on the date of the offer**. Quashes *White* (2d DCA); approves *Perez* (3d DCA); disapproves *Gulf Coast*, *Mincin*, *Williams*.
- This is the formula *CCM Condominium* (2021) declined to recede from and that Canady attacked there.
- ⭐⭐ **Footnote 1 is a Phase 1 lead — an actual 1990 staff analysis.** "[I]n **1990 the Legislature consolidated the offer of judgment statute with section 45.061** … **Fla. S. Comm. on Insurance, CS for SB 2670 Staff Analysis 7 (May 24, 1990)**. The Legislature expanded section 768.79 to cover both offers of settlement and judgment, and limited the applicability of section 45.061 to causes of action accruing before October 1, 1990."
  - ⚠️ **Chamber discrepancy to resolve:** the majority cites "**CS for SB 2670**"; Harding, J., dissenting, cites the same document as "**CS/HB 2670** (1990) Staff Analysis 7 (May 24, 1990)." One is wrong. **Pull the actual staff analysis** — it is the legislative history for ch. 90-119 and the best pre-2010 source found so far.
- ⭐ **Footnote 5 concedes the borrowing Canady later attacks:** "Although *Danis* and *Scottsdale* involved an award of attorneys' fees under **section 627.428**, we see no reason why this rationale should not apply equally to offers or demands made under section 768.79(6)." In *CCM* (2021) Canady says there is "no relevant textual similarity and thus no basis for applying the interpretation of one statute to the other." **The tension is visible on the face of *White* itself.**
- **Harding, J., partial dissent — "apples must be compared to apples."** If the offer excluded costs, the judgment threshold should exclude costs. Notes a district split on whether offers *must* include costs (3d DCA yes, per *Clinica Lourdes*; 4th DCA *Siedlecki* and 5th DCA *Hellmann* no). Would apply the new rule prospectively only, and would require all future offers to be inclusive of costs.
- **Arc:** #9 calculating "judgment obtained"

### ✅ D'Angelo v. Fitzmaurice, 863 So. 2d 311 (Fla. 2003) — ⚠️ peripheral

- **Author:** Bell, J. — Anstead, C.J., Wells, Pariente, Lewis, Quince, Cantero, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Overwhelmingly a setoff case** — economic vs. noneconomic damages under §§ 46.015(2), 768.041(2), 768.31(5) and the apportionment scheme of § 768.81, working through *Wells*, *Nash*, *Fabre*, and *Gouty*.
- **The § 768.79 portion is a single paragraph.** The certified conflict with *Spruce Creek* and *Flight Express* "was resolved by this Court in *Willis Shaw*," decided eight months earlier; the Court simply applies it and approves the Second District.
- ⚠️ **Fourth `citeCount` misranking.** 99 citations, essentially all for the setoff holding. Light-code it.
- **Still one useful data point:** the invalidated proposal was a **joint $250,000 offer by a husband and wife** (the Fitzmaurices) that did not apportion between them. That is a third spousal-apportionment casualty alongside *Hingson* and *Audiffred* — reinforcing Pariente's *Lamb* objection that the rule as construed reaches beyond *Fabre* to forbid the ordinary joint offer by spouses.
- **Arc:** #3 apportionment (application only)

## The foundation era, 1989–1996 — read

### ✅ Unicare Health Facilities, Inc. v. Mort, 553 So. 2d 159 (Fla. 1989) — ⭐ origin of the "purpose" pole

- **Author:** Barkett, J. — Ehrlich, C.J., Overton, McDonald, Shaw, Grimes, Kogan, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Posture:** a **pre-§ 768.79-era** case, decided under the old Fed. R. Civ. P. 68-style rule 1.442 (costs only).
- **Holding:** accepting an offer of judgment **silent as to attorney's fees terminates the litigation** and forecloses a later motion for statutory fees. Statutory fee rights **can be waived**. Quashes *Mort* (1st DCA); follows *Ahmed* and *George v. Northcraft* (5th DCA): there is an "organic right of parties to contract a settlement, which by definition concludes all claims unless the contract of settlement specifies otherwise."
- ⭐⭐ **The sentence that anchors thirty years of argument.** Rule 1.442 "was implemented **solely to encourage settlements in order to eliminate trials if possible**" (citing *Cheek v. McGowan Elec. Supply Co.*, 511 So. 2d 977, 981 (Fla. 1987)), and its intent was "to **terminate all claims, end disputes, and obviate the need for further intervention of the judicial process**."
  - This is quoted in *Security Professionals v. Segall* (4th DCA 1997), *Gorka* (2010), Pariente's *Lamb* and *Campbell* concurrences, and *Kuhajda* (2016). **It is the origin of the purposive pole against which the strict-construction line has been argued ever since.** Cite it from here, not from the later cases.
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance (purposive pole) · #8 contract formation

### ✅ Aspen v. Bayless, 564 So. 2d 1081 (Fla. 1990) — ⚠️ peripheral

- **Author:** McDonald, J. — Shaw, C.J., Overton, Ehrlich, Barkett, Grimes, Kogan, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Holding:** a party may recover costs under rule 1.442 even where its **insurer advanced or paid** them. The source of funds is irrelevant; subrogation sorts out the rest.
- ⚠️ **Another `citeCount` misranking** — 58 citations, mostly for the general costs/source-of-funds principle rather than offer-of-judgment doctrine. Light-code it.
- Useful policy language: denying costs "would **subvert the purpose and intent** of rule 1.442 and sections 45.061, and 768.79 … i.e., to encourage parties to settle claims without going to trial," and would leave "less incentive to accept an offer to settle and **no penalty for failing to do so**."
- **Arc:** costs mechanics only

### ✅ Leapai v. Milton, 595 So. 2d 12 (Fla. 1992)

- **Author:** Overton, J. — Shaw, C.J., McDonald, Barkett, Grimes, Kogan, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Holding:** § 45.061 is **constitutional** to the extent its procedural provisions have not been superseded by rule 1.442. Reverses *Milton v. Leapai*, 562 So. 2d 804 (Fla. 5th DCA 1990), which had struck the whole statute on the theory that its procedural aspects were **not severable**. The Court holds they **are** severable from the substantive fee right.
- ⭐ **The cooperation principle** — the constitutional frame for this entire field: "The judiciary and the legislature **must work to solve these types of separation-of-powers problems without encroaching upon each other's functions**." Models cited: the Florida Evidence Code (ch. 76-237 plus *In re Florida Evidence Code*, 372 So. 2d 1369 (Fla. 1979)) and *In re Rules of Civil Procedure*, 281 So. 2d 204 (Fla. 1973), where the Court adopted legislative enactments as rules, "recogniz[ing] the legislative action as a statement of the public desire."
- ⭐ **The retroactivity holding**, later load-bearing in *TGI Friday's* and *Sarkis*: "the right to recover attorney fees attaches **not to the cause of action, but to the unreasonable rejection of an offer of settlement**."
- Quotes 550 So. 2d 442 for the doubt that a percentage-based sanction is procedural "especially when a party may have done nothing more serious than **guessing wrong about a jury verdict**."
- ⭐ **Footnote 2 seeds the good-faith arc, in 1992:** "We do have some concern as to whether a **one dollar offer of settlement is a bona fide offer**. That issue was neither presented nor addressed in these proceedings."
- **Arc:** substance/procedure · #7 retroactivity · #6 good faith (seed)

### ✅ Timmons v. Combs, 608 So. 2d 1 (Fla. 1992) — ⭐⭐ the Phase 1 goldmine

- **Author:** Grimes, J. — Barkett, C.J., McDonald, Shaw, Kogan, Harding, JJ. **Overton, J., concurring, joined by McDonald, J.** Plus a **Supplemental Order, Oct. 22, 1992**.
- **Holding:** under § 45.061 a **defendant may recover** even where the defendant obtained the judgment, because that statute measures against "**the judgment entered**" and does not specify in whose favor. Quashes *Timmons* (1st DCA); approves *Memorial Sales* (3d DCA) and *Gross* (4th DCA); disapproves *Norris & Associates* and *Coe* (2d DCA).
- ⭐ **Pre-1990 § 768.79 did *not* permit a defendant to recover on a defense verdict**, because it measured against "the judgment obtained **by the plaintiff**." Courts uniformly so held: *Rabatie* (3d DCA), *Kline v. Publix* (2d DCA), *Oriental Imports* (5th DCA).
- ⭐⭐ **Two exact chapter-law citations — put these straight into Phase 1:**
  - **Ch. 90-119, § 48, Laws of Fla.** — the 1990 amendment "made clear that a defendant may now collect under that statute if his or her offer was rejected and the judgment is '**one of no liability**.'"
  - **Ch. 90-119, § 22, Laws of Fla.** — repealed § 45.061 as to causes of action accruing after **October 1, 1990**.
- **The Court repeals rule 1.442 outright** and adopts the **procedural portion of § 768.79 as a rule of Court**, effective the date of the opinion, inviting comments under Fla. R. Jud. Admin. 2.130(a).
- ✅ **Independently confirms the 550 So. 2d 442 correction:** "Because of the nonadversarial nature of the petition before us, **we declined to address the constitutionality** of the substantive portion of those statutes."
- ⭐⭐ **The Supplemental Order is the direct origin of the 1996 rewrite.** Commenters "lament the fact that the scope of section 768.79 is limited to '**civil actions for damages**'" and point to "perceived imperfections." The Court answers that these are "**beyond this Court's control because they are substantive in nature**," then asks the Civil Procedure Rules Committee to propose a new rule **outside the four-year cycle** — which becomes 682 So. 2d 105 (1996).
  - ⚠️ **The "civil actions for damages" limitation was flagged as a defect in 1992.** It is the exact issue litigated in *Nichols* (2006) and *Diamond Aircraft* (2013). **A twenty-one-year lag between the problem being identified and the Court resolving it — and it was resolved by construction, never by amendment.** Strong material for the memo.
- **Overton, J., concurring:** would have written § 768.79's provisions into a **new rule 1.442** rather than adopting the statute's procedural portion wholesale — "to assure a proper understanding of the operations of the court system."
- **Arc:** substance/procedure · statutory genealogy

### ✅ Knealing v. Puleo, 675 So. 2d 593 (Fla. 1996) — ⭐ the constitutional test

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Kogan, C.J., Shaw, Grimes, Harding, Anstead, JJ. **Overton, J., dissents** (without opinion).
- **Holding:** the mediation timing provisions of **§ 44.102(6), Fla. Stat. (1993), are unconstitutional**. They toll and alter the § 768.79 response periods but contain **no substantive fee authorization**, so they are **purely procedural** and intrude on art. V, § 2(a). Quashes *Puleo* (4th DCA); approves *Nordyne* (1st DCA); agrees with *Ong* (5th DCA).
- ⭐ **This supplies the operative constitutional test for the whole field.** *Leapai* and *Timmons* saved §§ 45.061 and 768.79 **because they contained substantive fee-authorizing provisions**. A statute in this area that is purely procedural does **not** survive. That is the cleanest statement of the substance/procedure line anywhere in the corpus.
- **"A statute providing authority to award fees must do so *expressly*"** (*Dorner v. Red Top Cab & Baggage Co.*, 37 So. 2d 160 (Fla. 1948)).
- Approves the Fourth District's holding that reasonableness of rejection is irrelevant to **entitlement**, consistent with *TGI Friday's*.
- ✅ **Third independent confirmation** of ch. 90-119, § 22 repealing § 45.061 as to post-Oct-1990 causes (n.5).
- **Arc:** substance/procedure · #10 timing

### ✅ Metropolitan Dade County v. Jones Boatyard, Inc., 611 So. 2d 512 (Fla. 1993) — ⭐ the versioning rule

- **Author:** Kogan, J. — Barkett, C.J., McDonald, Shaw, Grimes, Harding, JJ., concur. **Overton, J., dissents** (without opinion).
- ⭐⭐ **Holding:** § 768.79 does **not** apply to causes of action accruing before **July 1, 1986**, because § 768.71(2) provides that "[t]his part applies only to causes of action arising on or after July 1, 1986."
- ⭐ **The two statutes have different temporal triggers**, and this is the case that says so:
  - **§ 45.061** — the operative event is the **making of the offer** (*A.G. Edwards*, approved in *Leapai*): "the right to recover attorney fees attaches not to the cause of action, but to the unreasonable rejection of an offer."
  - **§ 768.79** — the right attaches to the **underlying cause of action**, because § 768.79 sits inside "an **integrated statutory scheme** under the negligence chapter" governed by § 768.71(2).
- ✅ **Adopts *Mudano*** (4th DCA 1989) — the very case flagged in Phase 0 as the origin of the retroactivity arc. **The Supreme Court ratified *Mudano*'s reasoning in 1993**, closing that loop.
- Rejects the argument that § 768.71(3) ("[i]f a provision of this part is in conflict with any other provision of the Florida Statutes, such other provision shall apply") lets § 45.061 substitute for § 768.79. Also rejects equitable estoppel — counsel "had equal access to the statutes and decisions."
- **Practical consequence for the memo:** every § 768.79 pin cite must be keyed to **the statutory version in effect when the cause of action accrued** — not when the offer was served, and not the current version. Critical because ch. 2022-271 renumbered the subsections.
- **Arc:** #7 retroactivity/effective date · versioning

### ✅ MX Investments, Inc. v. Crawford, 700 So. 2d 640 (Fla. 1997)

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Kogan, C.J., Overton, Shaw, Grimes, Harding, Anstead, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Holding:** § 768.79 supports fees only where a dismissal is **with prejudice**. "[O]nly when a plaintiff's voluntary dismissal is **with prejudice** or is a **second** voluntary dismissal is the defendant entitled to attorney fees." A rule 1.420(d) cost judgment is not a "judgment" for this purpose.
- Approves the First District's result but on **different reasoning** — not because no judgment was entered, but because the dismissal was without prejudice. Disapproves *Tampa Letter Carriers* (2d DCA) and *Special's Trading* (4th DCA).
- ⭐ **Phase 1 detail — the 1990 amendment's effective date.** Quoting the First District: "**Laws 1990, c. 90-119, § 48 (eff. October 1, 1990)**" rewrote § 768.79(1) **and** added subsection (6). Combined with *Timmons*, ch. 90-119 is now fully sourced: § 48 amended § 768.79 (eff. Oct. 1, 1990); § 22 repealed § 45.061 as to causes accruing after that date.
- **Arc:** #5 scope · #9 calculation

### ✅ Frosti v. Creel, 979 So. 2d 912 (Fla. 2008) — ⭐ resolves the *Hannah* appellate-fees question

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Lewis, C.J., Anstead, Pariente, Quince, Cantero, Bell, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Three holdings, all pro-offeror:**
  1. **Premature *filing* of a proposal does not bar fees.** Neither rule 1.442(d) nor § 768.79(3) says when a proposal may be filed, and "necessary to enforce" is undefined. The remedy for an unnecessary filing is a **motion to strike**, with leave to refile. Quashes *Frosti* (2d DCA), approves *Mills* (5th DCA), displacing *Bottcher* and *Browning*.
  2. **Rule 1.525 sets an outside deadline, not a window.** Per *Barco v. School Bd. of Pinellas County*, 975 So. 2d 1116 (Fla. 2008), the 30 days is a ceiling — a motion served *before* judgment is timely too.
  3. **"Judgment obtained" means the total net judgment**, not a slice of it. The defendant could not isolate the compensatory portion to defeat the threshold.
- ⭐⭐ **The appellate-fees question, answered — by Wells.** "**The right to attorney fees pursuant to section 768.79 applies to fees incurred on appeal**," citing only two DCA decisions (*Disney v. Vaughen*; *Westfield Ins. v. Mendolera*).
  - ⚠️ **This corrects the flag raised under *Hannah*.** In 1996, **Wells** wrote that "[a] plain reading of the statute **does not provide for attorney fees on appeal**, and the Court should not write such a provision into the statute." In 2008, **Wells writes the opinion holding the opposite** — in one sentence, on DCA authority, without acknowledging his earlier objection.
  - So *Coates* (2023) was **not** writing on a blank slate; *Frosti* settled it in 2008. But it was settled by a single unexplained sentence from the justice who had objected. **That is the finding worth reporting — not that the question went unanswered, but how thinly it was answered.**
- ⚠️ **Good-faith data point:** Frosti's second proposal offered **$1 to settle the punitive damages claim**, contingent on acceptance of the $24,999 total. No one challenged it as lacking good faith. Echoes *Leapai* n.2's 1992 doubt about "a one dollar offer of settlement."
- **Arc:** #10 timing · #9 calculation · #6 good faith

### ✅ Hannah v. Newkirk, 675 So. 2d 112 (Fla. 1996) — ⭐ Wells's access-to-courts and appellate-fees objections

- **Author:** Harding, J. — Kogan, C.J., Overton, Shaw, Anstead, JJ., concur. **Grimes, J., concurring. Wells, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part.**
- **Primary holding is a PIP question** — § 627.739(1) requires the elected PIP deductible to be set off from the verdict. The § 768.79 portion is consequential: once the setoff applied, the $2,401 offer exceeded the judgment by more than 25%, so entitlement followed. Restates the **two-prerequisite** test from *TGI Friday's*.
- ⭐ **Wells, J., dissenting in part — three objections that echo forward:**
  1. Adheres to his *TGI Friday's* position that § 768.79 was never meant to be **mandatory**; discretion should remain under (7)(b).
  2. ⭐ **Access to courts, art. I, § 21.** The mandatory reading "**impinges on the constitutional guarantee of access to courts**" — it forces a litigant to choose between abandoning a question the district court certified as one of great public importance and "pursuing it upon the gamble that his small judgment will be eviscerated." **This is the access-to-courts objection raised from the bench in 1996 — a decade before *Nichols* (2006) rejected the same argument in the PIP context.**
  3. ⭐ **He counts *three* prerequisites, not two** — the *TGI Friday's* formulation omits § 768.79(6)'s requirement that the motion be made **within thirty days**. And: "**A plain reading of the statute does not provide for attorney fees on appeal**, and the Court should not write such a provision into the statute."
     - ⚠️ **Check against *Coates* (2023)**, which awarded **appellate** fees without engaging this objection. Wells raised it in 1996; the Court resolved it the other way 27 years later. If *Coates* never addresses it, that is a clean example of the Court deciding by omission.
- **Grimes, J., concurring** — agrees with Wells that it "makes more sense" to let the judge weigh the (7)(b) factors in deciding *whether* to award a fee, "[u]nfortunately, **the wording of the statute does not permit this interpretation**." A second justice signalling the statute compels a result he thinks unwise.
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance · #6 good faith/discretion · #9 calculation

### ✅ Gulliver Academy, Inc. v. Bodek, 694 So. 2d 675 (Fla. 1997)

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Overton, Shaw, Grimes, Harding, Anstead, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Holding:** the 30-day period in § 768.79(6) is **procedural**, governed by the rules, and may be enlarged under rule 1.090(b). **A reservation of jurisdiction in a final judgment is procedurally an enlargement of time.** "Any other interpretation would make the trial court's reservation in the final judgment not only a **nullity** but a **procedural trap**."
- No showing of excusable neglect is needed where the reservation was made **before** the 30 days ran; excusable neglect is required only if it comes after. Quashes *Bodek* (3d DCA); approves *Gilbert* (1st DCA) in part, disapproves it on the excusable-neglect point.
- Policy: "Issues related to setting of fees under section 768.79 are **not fully adjudicated until the disposal of posttrial motions**."
- ⭐ **The rule/statute divergence, stated plainly.** As revised in 1996, rule **1.442(g)** differs from § 768.79(6) — it runs the 30 days from **entry of judgment in a nonjury action** but from **return of the verdict in a jury action**. "Since these time requirements are procedural, **the rule prevails where it differs from the statute**." (682 So. 2d 105, 124–25.)
- ✅ Fourth independent confirmation of **ch. 90-119, § 22** repealing § 45.061 (n.1).

#### ⭐ How this squares with *Saia* (2006) — Wells wrote both

Pariente's *Saia* dissent accused the majority of overruling *Gulliver Academy* **sub silentio**. Reading both, the distinction Wells draws is real but narrow: ***Gulliver* construed the statute's own 30-day period**, before rule 1.525 existed; ***Saia* construed rule 1.525**, adopted in 2000, which set a bright-line deadline the rules had not previously contained. Same author, nine years apart, opposite results on nearly the same facts — reconcilable only because an intervening rule changed the governing text.

**This is a clean, self-contained illustration of the project's central mechanism:** the rule and the statute govern the same conduct on different terms, and which one controls determines the outcome.

#### ⚠️ Promotes a Tier B case

Footnote 2 flags ***Metropolitan Dade County v. Jones Boatyard*, 611 So. 2d 512 (Fla. 1993)**, which held that "the applicable version of **section 768.79** is the version in effect **at the time the cause of action accrues**, while the applicable version of **section 45.061(2)** is the version in effect **at the time the offer was unreasonably rejected**."

That answers *which statutory version applies* — directly load-bearing for the versioning discipline already flagged (ch. 2022-271 renumbered the subsections). **Promoted from Tier B to Tier A.**

- **Arc:** #10 timing/deadlines · substance/procedure

### ✅ Sarkis v. Allstate Ins. Co., 863 So. 2d 210 (Fla. 2003) — ⭐⭐ the master source for Phase 2

- **Author:** Per curiam — Anstead, C.J., Lewis, Quince, Bell, JJ. **Wells, J., concurring, joined by Bell, J. Pariente, J., dissenting.** Cantero, J., recused.
- **Holding:** a **contingency risk multiplier may not be used** in calculating fees under § 768.79 and rule 1.442. Approves *Sarkis* (5th DCA en banc); **disapproves _Pirelli Armstrong_ (2d DCA), _Island Hoppers_ (4th DCA), and _Lewis v. Bondy_ (1st DCA)** — a 1-versus-3 district alignment again.
- **Three grounds:**
  1. § 768.79 fees are **sanctions/penalties** for unreasonable rejection, not an inducement to secure counsel.
  2. *Quanstrom* and *Rowe* authorized multipliers to promote **access to courts** by inducing lawyers to take cases **at the inception**. By the time an offer is rejected, counsel has already been retained, so the multiplier's purpose is inconsistent with the statute's.
  3. **Strict construction**, with a second canon: "a statute imposing a **penalty** must be strictly construed in favor of the one against whom the penalty is imposed and **is never extended by construction**" (*Hotel & Restaurant Comm'n v. Sunny Seas No. One*, 104 So. 2d 570 (Fla. 1958)).
- **Wells, J., concurring** supplies the textual hooks later used in *Coates*: "§ 768.79(1) expressly states that the attorney fees are '**penalties**.' Rule 1.442(g) expressly states that the fees are '**sanctions**.'"
- **Pariente, J., dissenting:** contingency falls within "all other relevant criteria" in § 768.79(7)(b); the prospect of a multiplier can be precisely why counsel took the case; and it aids settlement because a defendant must price that risk.

1. **550 So. 2d 442 (1989) did *not* hold the statutes unconstitutional.** The earlier note recorded that it did — that came from the *trial court's* characterization quoted in *TGI Friday's*. *Sarkis* is explicit: the Court "**declined to address the statutes' constitutionality in the nonadversarial rules proceeding**." 2. **§ 45.061 was enacted in 1987, not 1986**, and was **repealed prospectively in 1990** as to causes accruing after Oct. 1, 1990. Together with *CCM Condominium* (2021), the full picture: enacted 1987 → prospectively repealed 1990 → survives only for pre-Oct-1990 causes of action → still on the books as § 45.061, Fla. Stat. (2020).

### ✅ Saia Motor Freight Line, Inc. v. Reid, 930 So. 2d 598 (Fla. 2006)

- **Author:** Wells, J. — Quince, Cantero, Bell, JJ., concur. **Pariente, C.J., dissenting, joined by Anstead and Lewis, JJ. 4–3.**
- **Holding:** rule 1.525's 30-day deadline to serve a motion for costs and fees is a **bright-line** requirement; a **reservation of jurisdiction** in the final judgment does not extend it. Quashes *Saia* (3d DCA); disapproves *Fisher* (4th DCA); approves *Gulf Landings* (2d), *Wentworth* (5th), *Southtrust Bank* (1st).
- Effectively confines ***Gulliver Academy v. Bodek*, 694 So. 2d 675 (Fla. 1997)**, which had held a reservation of jurisdiction was an enlargement of time under rule 1.090(b).
- **Rule genealogy detail:** rule **1.442(g)** was adopted in 1996 imposing a 30-day sanctions-motion deadline; after rule **1.525** was adopted in 2000 (773 So. 2d 1098), **1.442(g) was amended to conform to 1.525**.
- **Pariente, C.J., dissenting:** the majority converts a reservation of jurisdiction into the very "nullity" and "procedural trap" *Gulliver Academy* condemned. Invokes ***Puryear v. State***, 810 So. 2d 901, 905 (Fla. 2002): "this Court does not intentionally overrule itself **sub silentio**." ### ✅ Southeast Floating Docks, Inc. v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co., 82 So. 3d 73 (Fla. 2012)

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Canady, C.J., Pariente, Quince, Polston, Labarga, JJ., concur. **Perry, J., dissents. 6–1.**
- **Posture:** three questions certified by the Eleventh Circuit. The Court answers **only the third**, rendering the first two moot.
- **Holding:** § 768.79 is **substantive for both constitutional *and* conflict-of-law purposes**. Where parties have contractually agreed to be bound by another state's substantive law, **§ 768.79 does not apply**.
- **Disapproves _BDO Seidman_ (4th DCA 2001) and _Bennett v. Morales_ (5th DCA 2003)**, which read "[i]n any civil action for damages filed in the courts of this state" to compel application regardless of choice of law. The rebuke is quotable: "**If the statute actually stated this, its plain reading would supersede the choice of law by the parties. But it does not.**"
- **Choice-of-law framework:** *Mazzoni Farms*, 761 So. 2d 306 (Fla. 2000) — enforce a choice-of-law clause unless it contravenes a strong Florida public policy. § 768.79 is not materially different from § 57.105 for this purpose (*Walls v. Quick & Reilly*; *Precision Tune Auto Care*).
- **Substantive/procedural test:** *Massey v. David*, 979 So. 2d 931, 936–37 (Fla. 2008), quoting *Haven Fed. Sav. & Loan v. Kirian*, 579 So. 2d 730 (Fla. 1991).
- ✅ **Confirms the 1972 origin** — "the origins of Florida's offer of judgment statute date back to **1972**, when this Court initially adopted a variation … that **mirrored Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 68**," citing *In re Fla. Bar*, 265 So. 2d 21. **Remove that entry from the probable-false-positive list.**
- Repeats the *Gorka* admission: the statute and rule "have produced a significant amount of independent litigation."
- **Arc:** #5 scope · conflict of laws · substance/procedure

### ✅ CCM Condominium Ass'n v. Petri Positive Pest Control, No. SC19-861 (Fla. Sept. 9, 2021) — ⭐ completes the Canady thread

- **Author:** Polston, J. — Labarga, Muñiz, Couriel, Grosshans, JJ., concur. **Canady, C.J., dissents, joined by Lawson, J. 5–2.**
- **Holding:** **post-offer prejudgment interest is excluded** from the "judgment obtained" compared against a rejected offer. Approves *Petri* (4th DCA); disapproves *Perez* (3d DCA 1998) and *Phillips* (1st DCA 1991) — both pre-*White*.
- **The *White* formula** (*White v. Steak & Ale*, 816 So. 2d 546 (Fla. 2002)): "judgment obtained" = net judgment + attorney's fees + taxable costs + prejudgment interest **as they stood on the date of the offer**. Reaffirmed in *Nichols* (2006) and *Shands* (2012).
- **Method: stare decisis, not text.** The Court applies *State v. Poole*, 297 So. 3d 487, 507 (Fla. 2020) — precedent yields only when "clearly erroneous" — and finds no "definite and firm conviction" that *White* is wrong. ⚠️ **The Fourth District below had said the plain meaning of § 768.79 does *not* support the precedent, but felt bound by it.** A majority of the Court then declined to look.

#### ⭐⭐ Canady's dissent — the theory behind a decade of his separate writings

- **Holding vs. assumption.** Quoting **Bryan A. Garner et al., *The Law of Judicial Precedent* (2016)**: "Not all text within a judicial decision serves as precedent. That's a role generally reserved only for holdings…" and "**The assumptions a court uses to reach a particular result do not themselves create a new precedent or strengthen existing precedent.**"
- *White*'s question presented concerned only **pre-offer taxable costs**. Everything it said about post-offer fees, costs, and interest was assumption, not holding — and in *White* the threshold was crossed on pre-offer costs alone, so the point was never live. *Shands* and *Nichols* likewise never decided it.
- ⭐ **The *White* formula's authorities were § 627.428 cases** — a structurally different statute. "There is no relevant textual similarity and thus no basis for applying the interpretation of one statute to the other statute. **The pertinent statements from *White* thus are of very dubious provenance.**"
- **The § 45.061 comparison.** That statute *does* define "the amount of the judgment" to include costs "prior to the making of the offer," § 45.061(2)(b). So the Legislature knows how to exclude post-offer amounts; its omission from § 768.79 was deliberate. Cites *Cason*, 944 So. 2d 306 (Fla. 2006) and *Olmstead v. FTC*, 44 So. 3d 76 (Fla. 2010).
- ⭐ **Answers a Phase 1 question: § 45.061 was never repealed.** Canady cites "§ 45.061, Florida Statutes (**2020**), which applies to offers of settlement for causes of action that accrued on or before the effective date of the statute in 1990." It remains on the books but is **vestigial** — reaching only pre-1990 causes of action. Pariente's "only one statute" remark in *Campbell* was practical, not literal. **Correct the Phase 1 checklist accordingly.**

#### The fourth genealogy — and Canady's unifying theory

*CCM* supplies what the *Pratt* and *Audiffred* dissents lacked: an **explicit theory**. Canady's consistent objection across a decade is that **the Court repeatedly treats broad statements as holdings and extends them past what was actually decided** — voiced as a jurisdictional bar in *Pratt*/*Audiffred* (2015), as a precedent-scope argument here (2021), and as the merits carve-out he authored in *Kuhajda* (2016).

⭐ ***Trace Elements* (2026) runs on this exact framework.** Sasso, J., dissenting, cites *Pedroza v. State*, 291 So. 3d 541, 547 (Fla. 2020), which in turn quotes ***State v. Yule*, 905 So. 2d 251, 259 n.10 (Fla. 2d DCA 2005) (Canady, J., specially concurring)** — Canady's own pre-Supreme Court writing on what constitutes a holding. **The 2026 dissent is built on a definition of "holding" that Canady authored as a district judge in 2005 and deployed against this very line of cases in 2021.**

- **Arc:** #9 calculation of "judgment obtained" · precedent scope

### ✅ Suarez Trucking FL Corp. v. Souders, No. SC21-369 (Fla. Oct. 20, 2022) — a new arc

- **Author:** Per curiam — Muñiz, C.J., Canady, Polston, Couriel, Grosshans, JJ. **Canady, J., concurs with an opinion, joined by Polston, J. Labarga, J., dissents with an opinion. Francis, J., did not participate. 5–1.**
- **Holding:** a filed written notice of acceptance under § 768.79(4) **forms a binding settlement contract**. Acceptance is not performance. Quashes *Suarez Trucking*, 311 So. 3d 263 (Fla. 2d DCA 2020); approves *Cirrus Design Corp. v. Sasso*, 95 So. 3d 308 (Fla. 4th DCA 2012).
- The Second District had applied the **"mirror image" rule** to require the acceptance to recite the offer's terms back. The Court: "This is a rule of **consistency**. It is not — as the Second District would have it — a rule of **regurgitation**." The district court "cites not a single case in which the mirror-image rule has been applied in a similar way."
- **Framework language worth quoting:** "Once a proper acceptance — that is, an unqualified acceptance — is filed as specified in the statute, **that's it**: a settlement contract has been entered… All that remains is for performance." And: "the statutory framework **does not envision a process of negotiation** regarding settlement terms. On the contrary, it authorizes settlement proposals that are **by their very nature take-it-or-leave-it propositions**."
- Rule 1.442(f)(1) — "[n]o oral communications shall constitute an acceptance, rejection, or counteroffer" — makes surrounding oral discussions irrelevant to formation. Per *Scope v. Fannelli*, 639 So. 2d 141 (Fla. 5th DCA 1994), **a counteroffer does not terminate a § 768.79 offer**; only a written withdrawal under (5) or expiry of the 30 days does.
- **Canady, J., concurring:** statutory incorporation — laws existing when a contract is made are read into it (*City of Homestead v. Beard*, 600 So. 2d 450 (Fla. 1992); *Von Hoffman v. City of Quincy*, 71 U.S. 535 (1866)). Applied to the workers' compensation lien under § 440.39, he doubts any breach occurred.
- **Labarga, J., dissenting:** § 768.79 and rule 1.442 govern *court approval and enforcement*, not contract **formation**, which is governed by general contract law. No meeting of the minds here on a material term (who appears as payee on the settlement check). Warns the majority "risks minimizing the safeguards of contract law in favor of a **purely formalistic framework**."

#### ⭐ The valence flips

Here a **textually restrained** reading of § 768.79 **defeats** an extra-textual requirement (mirror-image regurgitation) and **saves** the settlement. That is the opposite of *Hingson*/*Gorka*, where extra-textual requirements were **added** to invalidate proposals.

**Same statute, opposite methods, depending on which provision is at issue:** the Court reads the *acceptance* provisions with textual restraint (2022) and the *offer* provisions with extra-textual strictness (2002–2026). Worth a section of its own.

⚠️ Note also that **Labarga dissents here arguing for a common-law overlay on the statute**, then joins the *Trace Elements* majority applying rigid rule enforcement four years later. Check whether those positions are reconcilable before characterizing his views.

- **Arc:** #8 contract formation and acceptance mechanics (new)

### ✅ Anderson v. Hilton Hotels Corp., 202 So. 3d 846 (Fla. 2016) — ⭐⭐ strict construction, running the other way

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Labarga, C.J., Pariente, Quince, Perry, JJ., concur. **Canady and Polston, JJ., concur in result.** Unanimous in result.
- **Timing:** decided **November 3, 2016 — two weeks after *Kuhajda*** (Oct. 20, 2016). Same Court, same term.
- **Facts:** Anderson served four separate, individually-worded proposals (Hilton $650k, W2007 $100k, Interstate $650k, SecurAmerica $300k). His wife served her own separate consortium offers, then voluntarily dismissed. At trial the parties agreed to call three defendants "Embassy Suites" for the jury's convenience; the judgment came back unapportioned among them.

#### Four holdings, all pro-offeror

1. **Anti-nitpicking.** The Fifth District's reading — that "PLAINTIFF" might include the wife — was "**unreasonable and in contravention of this Court's direction in *Nichols*.**" Courts are "**discouraged from 'nitpicking' proposals for settlement to search for ambiguity**" (*Carey-All Transp. v. Newby*, 989 So. 2d 1201, 1206 (Fla. 2d DCA 2008)). The conclusion is quotable: the courts below "**unnecessarily injected ambiguity into these proceedings and created more judicial labor, not less**."
2. **A single offeror need not address a co-plaintiff's separate claim.** Resolves conflict in favor of *Miley v. Nash* (2d DCA), *Alamo Financing v. Mazoff* (4th DCA), and *Land & Sea Petroleum* (4th DCA).
3. ⭐ **The precise line between this and *Audiffred*.** *Audiffred*'s proposal recited that it resolved "[a]ny and all claims **Plaintiffs** have brought" and expressly required **both** spouses to dismiss — that is what made it a joint proposal. Anderson's used the singular "PLAINTIFF," defined as Troy alone, and never mentioned Paula. "**Apportionment simply was not an issue below.**"
   - **This is the operative drafting rule** and belongs at the top of the practitioner checklist: a proposal becomes "joint" when it *speaks for* or *binds* another party, not merely because another party has claims pending.
4. ⭐ **Offers may not be aggregated.** Adopting *Hess v. Walton* (2d DCA 2005) and *Thornburg v. Pursell* (2d DCA 1985), separate offers cannot be summed and compared against the judgment. Also: entitlement does not require a judgment against a *designated* offeree — "[t]his entitlement is only dependent upon a sufficient offer and judgment obtained, **not a judgment 'obtained from designated offerees.'**"

#### ⭐⭐ The valence flips again — and this is now a pattern

> "The only way that these offers could not satisfy the statutory requirement would be if the offers were to be aggregated, **which cannot be tolerated under a strict construction of section 768.79**."

**Here strict construction *favors the offeror* and *saves* the fee award.** That is the second confirmed instance, alongside *Suarez Trucking* (2022), where textual strictness produces a pro-offeror result.

**Revise the framing accordingly:** strict construction is not inherently pro-offeree. It cuts against whichever side is asking the Court to read something into the text. In *Hingson*/*Gorka* the offeree wanted an unwritten apportionment requirement read *in*; in *Anderson* and *Suarez Trucking* the offeree wanted aggregation and a mirror-image rule read *in*. **The Court refuses additions when they would defeat a fee award just as readily as it makes them when they would.** That inconsistency — not strictness itself — is the real target.

#### Also worth keeping

- Quotes *Hess* on the legitimate strategy behind differentiated offers: it "forces one defendant to settle," lets the plaintiff "eliminate the defendant for whom the jury may have sympathy," or "remove the defendant with the best lawyer." Candid, and useful for the good-faith arc.
- **Motive is irrelevant to validity.** Even if counsel admitted the offer was meant to settle both spouses' claims, "[t]he underlying motives of a party that proposes settlement are unrelated to the strict requirements of the … statute and rule." Motive goes to **§ 768.79(7) good faith**, not to facial validity.
- Reaffirms the *TGI Friday's*/*Schmidt* "mandatory right" framing; the only escape is a (7)(a) bad-faith finding.
- **Canady and Polston concur in result only** — consistent with their decade-long refusal to join this line's reasoning.
- **Arc:** #1 strict compliance (pro-offeror) · #3 apportionment (boundary) · #4 ambiguity · #6 good faith · #9 calculation

### ✅ Koppel v. Ochoa, 243 So. 3d 886 (Fla. 2018) — ⭐ the Court's answer to the rule-1.010 argument

- **Author:** Quince, J. — Labarga, C.J., Pariente, Lewis, Canady, Polston, Lawson, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Holding:** filing a rule 1.090 motion to enlarge time does **not** toll the 30-day acceptance period under rule 1.442(f)(1). Approves *Ochoa* (2d DCA); **disapproves *Goldy v. Corbett Cranes*** (5th DCA 1997), which had been the controlling authority for **19 years**.
- Practical stakes: the offeree filed the motion one day before expiry, the hearing was two months later, and she purported to accept **90 days after** the offer was made. "The rules do not support this outcome." Otherwise "an offeree could extend the offer **indefinitely**, all while the offering party continues to incur costs."
- ⭐⭐ **The Court confronts the rule-1.010 argument head-on — and rejects it on threshold grounds.** Petitioner urged the Court to construe the rules using rule 1.010's "just, speedy, and inexpensive" directive:
  > "However, **this is the direction only if a rule needs interpretation**. Here, the language is clear and unambiguous. Accordingly, **there is no need to resort to the rules of statutory interpretation, which would require us to consider the purpose of the rules as outlined in rule 1.010**."
  - **This is the Court's answer to the Bell/Sasso thread.** Bell in *Campbell* (2007) and Sasso in *Trace Elements* (2026) both argue rule 1.010 should govern instead of the derogation canon. *Koppel* holds rule 1.010 is available **only if the text is ambiguous** — a textualist answer that neutralizes *both* canons at the threshold.
  - ⚠️ **The memo must engage this.** The rule-1.010 argument is weaker than the *Trace Elements* dissent presents it, because a unanimous Court in 2018 said the predicate is ambiguity — and *Trace Elements* treats (c)(3) as unambiguous.
- **Prospective application rejected**, and the reasoning is worth noting: a DCA decision is not an "overruling decision" by a "court of last resort," so the *Strickland*/*Lockwood* prospectivity exception has no application. "Rules 1.090 and 1.442 **do not, and did not**, provide for tolling."
  - Pattern: Harding sought prospectivity in *White* (2002) and Pariente in *Saia* (2006); both lost. In *Koppel* the Court rejects it unanimously. **This line has never been applied prospectively.**
- **Drafting trap worth the checklist:** rule 1.442(f)(1) expressly provides that Fla. R. Jud. Admin. 2.514(b) — the extra-days-for-service provision — **does not apply**.
- **Arc:** #10 timing/deadlines · #8 acceptance mechanics

### ✅ Allen v. Nunez, 258 So. 3d 1207 (Fla. 2018) — ⭐⭐ three justices call the doctrine unpredictable

- **Author:** Lewis, J. — Pariente, Quince, Labarga, JJ., concur; **Pariente, J., concurring with an opinion. Polston, J., dissenting, joined by Canady, C.J., and Lawson, J. 4–3.**
- **Holding:** separate, individually-named proposals to two codefendants (the driver and his vicariously liable father) were **unambiguous**, despite a paragraph 5 reciting the proposal was "inclusive of all damages claimed by Plaintiff." Quashes *Nunez v. Allen* (5th DCA 2016).
- **Follows *Anderson* directly** — the proposals used a nearly identical drafting template. Resolves conflict with seven decisions: *Anderson* (Fla.), *Miley* and *Cassidy* (2d DCA), *Kiefer*, *Llanio-Gonzalez*, *Alamo Financing*, and *Land & Sea Petroleum* (4th DCA).
- **Interpretive rule:** "any potential ambiguity in paragraph 5 is resolved by examining the proposal for settlement **as a whole**" (*Moore v. State Farm*, 916 So. 2d 871 (Fla. 2d DCA 2005)). And bluntly: "it appears **disingenuous** to assert that there exists a legitimate question as to whether one codefendant's acceptance could have settled the offeror's claim against the other codefendant."
- Repeats the *Anderson* line: the nitpicking "unnecessarily injected ambiguity into these proceedings and **created more judicial labor, not less**."

#### ⭐⭐ The quotable admission

**Polston, J., dissenting — joined by Canady, C.J., and Lawson, J.** — declines jurisdiction, and in doing so states that this Court's jurisprudence

> "**in this area of the law seems inconsistently applied and unpredictable**"

— but adds, "[e]ven if there are other cases … in conflict, **I would not exercise jurisdiction**."

**Three sitting justices, in 2018, describing the Court's own proposal-for-settlement jurisprudence as inconsistent and unpredictable.** This is the single most useful sentence found so far for the memo's diagnostic section — it is not an advocate's characterization but the Court's own.

The majority answers in footnote 2: "the purpose of conflict review is the **elimination of inconsistent views** about the same question of law … it is paramount this Court exercise jurisdiction to eliminate any inconsistencies in this area of law."

#### The Canady–Polston jurisdictional bloc, confirmed

*Pratt* (2015), *Audiffred* (2015), *Allen* (2018) — dissenting on **jurisdictional** grounds each time; *Anderson* (2016) — concurring in result only. ### ✅ Coates v. R.J. Reynolds Tobacco Co., 365 So. 3d 353 (Fla. 2023) — ⭐ the method problem

- **Author:** Grosshans, J. — Muñiz, C.J., Canady, Couriel, Francis, JJ., concur. Labarga, J., concurs in result. **Sasso, J., did not participate.**
- **Holding:** § 768.79 is **not a prevailing-party statute**. A party need not prevail to be entitled to fees.
- **Two textual grounds:**
  1. **The statute calls its own awards "penalties"** — "except for pursuing the **penalties** of this section," § 768.79(1). All five DCAs have so characterized it.
  2. The statute **contemplates awards to non-prevailing litigants** — a defendant can recover even where the plaintiff wins a substantial judgment.
- Contrasts § 768.79 with real prevailing-party statutes: § 59.46, § 57.105(7), § 627.428(1). "Had the Legislature intended for section 768.79 to be a prevailing-party statute, it could have adopted similar language … but it did not."
- Rejects the "flood of frivolous appeals" worry: reasonableness is policed by the § 768.79(8)(b) factors, and a party may still argue an appeal was frivolous.

#### ⭐⭐ The methodological finding — *Coates* undercuts the line that cites it

*Coates* is the modern Court's declared interpretive method, and it is **not the derogation canon**. The canon is never mentioned. Instead:

> "we apply the **supremacy-of-the-text principle**, recognizing that '[t]he words of a governing text are of paramount concern, and what they convey, in their context, is what the text means'" (quoting *Levy v. Levy*, 326 So. 3d 678 (Fla. 2021); *Page v. Deutsche Bank*, 308 So. 3d 953 (Fla. 2020)) — and "**we do not add words to a statute in the guise of interpreting it**" (*Statler v. State*, 349 So. 3d 873 (Fla. 2022)).

***Trace Elements* cites *Coates* for the "penalty statute" premise** to justify strict enforcement. But *Coates*'s actual method cuts the other way:

- *Hingson* **added words** — an apportionment requirement nowhere stated in "[n]ame the party making it and the party to whom it is being made."
- *Gorka* **added words** — an independent-acceptance requirement the majority conceded it derived because precedent "inherently requires" it.

On *Coates*'s own stated methodology, both are exactly the move the Court says it does not make. **The modern Court's textualism is in tension with the apportionment line it inherited** — and *Trace Elements* borrows *Coates*'s label ("penalty") while declining its method.

- ⭐ **§ 627.428 repeal pinned down.** Footnote 2: "This statute has since been repealed. See **ch. 2023-15, § 11, Laws of Fla.** (effective date of **March 24, 2023**)." That closes the *Nichols*/Anstead thread — the Legislature answered his 2006 call to restore the insured's one-way street by abolishing it.
- ⚠️ **Subsection renumbering.** *Coates* cites the good-faith and reasonableness provisions as **§ 768.79(7)–(8)** and the factors as **(8)(b)**; pre-2022 cases cite them as **(7)(a)** and **(7)(b)**. **Ch. 2022-271 renumbered the subsections.** Every pin cite in the memo must be tied to a statutory year. ## The separate dissents — read, with two corrections

### *Gorka* — Polston, J., dissenting, joined by Quince, C.J., and Canady, J.

⚠️ **Correction: *Gorka* is not an apportionment case.** The dissent reproduces the actual proposal, and it **fully apportioned** — "$12,500.00 to Plaintiff, John W. Gorka, and a payment of $12,500.00 to Plaintiff, Laurel Lee Larson." The sole defect was the **mutual-acceptance condition**. *Gorka* therefore belongs under conditions/(c)(2)(C) and the judicially created independent-acceptance requirement, **not** under (c)(3) apportionment. Reclassified above.

Polston's argument, which is substantive and detailed:
- **No prohibition exists in the text.** Neither rule 1.442 nor § 768.79 forbids a mutual-acceptance condition. Both offerees were identified; the terms were unambiguous and individually addressed. The plain language is satisfied.
- **The majority's precedents don't fit.** *Lamb* and *Behar* both concerned **failures to apportion**; neither addressed a mutual-acceptance condition. (The same factual-distinguishability objection Lewis made in *Lamb* and Sasso makes in *Trace Elements*.)
- ⭐ **A concrete mechanism refuting the majority's central worry.** The majority feared a willing offeree could be exposed to the fee sanction through the other offeree's refusal. Polston walks the statute: § 768.79(4) and rule 1.442(f)(1) let the willing offeree file a written notice of acceptance; § 768.79(6)(a) permits recovery only where the offer "is not accepted by the plaintiff." So a plaintiff who files acceptance is **protected**, even though the court would lack jurisdiction to enforce a settlement absent both acceptances. The majority dismissed this as "flawed logic" and a "phantom offer" without engaging the statutory text.
- **The practical case:** "In many instances, a party is motivated to settle an entire case with all parties … But if the case is going to continue, then there may be little incentive to partially settle. The only way then to settle these cases is to make joint offers conditioned on all accepting." Conditioned joint offers therefore *encourage* settlement.
- "**To interpret these provisions any other way effectively eliminates the ability to make joint offers.**"
- Quotes *Clements* (1st DCA): "Rule 1.442 is designed to **facilitate** settlements, not to render settlement of a case impossible where there are multiple defendants."

**The Legislature adopted Polston's position** twelve years later in § 768.79(6) (ch. 2022-271).

### *Pratt* — Canady, J., dissenting, joined by Polston, J. — **jurisdictional only**

### *Audiffred* — Canady, J., dissenting, joined by Polston, J. — **jurisdictional only**

⚠️ **Correction: the hypothesis that Canady's 2015 dissents share a merits theory was wrong.** Both are short and purely jurisdictional — he would **dismiss for lack of express and direct conflict** under art. V, § 3(b)(3), and reaches no merits question in either.

- ***Pratt***: the Fourth District found a **single entity**, not multiple offerors — same lawyer, single answer, single listing on the verdict form, and the parties ultimately agreed FMC Hospital, Ltd. was the only proper defendant. So no conflict on similar controlling facts with *Willis Shaw* or *Lamb*.
- ***Audiffred***: *Frey*, *Eastern Atlantic*, and *Alioto-Alexander* all involved proposals found to be by a **sole offeror**; the First District distinguished them because Audiffred's proposal "promise[d] that another individual would take affirmative action upon acceptance." No conflict on the same controlling facts.
  - ⭐ That distinction — **a promise that a non-party will act, versus a mere condition that the offeree release a non-party** — is a real doctrinal line worth carrying into the practitioner checklist.

### The corrected Canady thread

Not "consistent internal skeptic on the merits." The accurate version is narrower and better:

**Canady's consistent objection is that the Court keeps reaching down to extend apportionment doctrine into factually different scenarios.** In *Pratt* and *Audiffred* (2015) he deploys that as a **jurisdictional** bar — no conflict on similar facts, so no power to review. In *Kuhajda* (2016), where conflict *was* properly certified and jurisdiction was secure, he had the vehicle and wrote the **merits** carve-out. He had earlier joined Polston's substantive *Gorka* dissent (2010) and dissented in part in *Diamond Aircraft* (2013), and later concurred in result in *Wheaton* (2019).

The through-line is factual distinguishability — the same complaint Lewis raised in *Lamb* and Sasso raises in *Trace Elements* — but Canady raises it as a limit on the Court's **jurisdiction**, which is a distinct and sharper claim.

**Therefore:** *Trace Elements* is doctrinally faithful to *Kuhajda* — (c)(3) does implement what *Hingson* and *Gorka* say the statute requires — but the premise it inherits is the weakest link in the chain, and the institutional history suggests it will not survive contact with the rulemaking process either.

***Wheaton* remains unreconciled.** Its alternative holding applies the substantive-compliance test to a *different* rule set (2.516) — broader than anything *Kuhajda* did — and the *Trace Elements* majority does not mention it.

## Emerging finding — the pendulum is real and datable

Seven read cases now date the arc precisely:

**1995 *TGI Friday's*** — the Court is *permissive*. Entitlement is arithmetic; reasonableness of rejection is irrelevant. **Wells, J., writing separately, first argues for strict construction** as a derogation-of-common-law and penalty statute.
**2003 *Willis Shaw*** — **Wells now writes for the Court**, importing the derogation canon from statutes to the *rule*, citing only statute cases and offering no separate justification for the extension.
**2006 *Nichols* → 2007 *Campbell* → 2013 *Diamond Aircraft*** — strict construction hardens. "All portions must be strictly construed." *Diamond Aircraft* cites *TGI Friday's* at **615** — a page inside Wells's 1995 separate opinion — completing the conversion of dissent into black letter.
**2016 *Kuhajda* → 2019 *Wheaton*** — **not** an inversion (see correction below). A narrow carve-out for rule provisions that do **not** implement a substantive requirement of § 768.79. *Kuhajda* revives *Bennett* and confines *Diamond Aircraft*.
**2026 *Trace Elements*** — 4–3. Applies *Kuhajda*'s own test and finds (c)(3) substantive-implementing; ***Wheaton* never addressed**.

### ⚠️ Correction — the earlier "pendulum inversion" framing was too broad

Reading *Kuhajda* rather than inferring it changes the thesis. *Kuhajda* is **unanimous in result**, and it does not reject strict construction or the derogation canon. It asks a single question: **does the rule provision implement a substantive requirement of § 768.79?** If yes, strict construction applies; if no, the rule cannot be construed to defeat the statute.

On that test, ***Trace Elements* is faithful to *Kuhajda*, not a departure from it.** The majority finds rule 1.442(c)(3) substantive-implementing because *Gorka* and *Hingson* read § 768.79(2)(b)'s singular "party" to require apportionment — so strict construction follows on *Kuhajda*'s own terms.

### The revised central question — sharper and more defensible

The load-bearing premise is not the canon. It is the **statutory** reading underneath it:

> **Does § 768.79(2)(b)'s requirement that a proposal "name the party making it and the party to whom it is being made" really impose an apportionment requirement — such that rule 1.442(c)(3) "implements a substantive requirement" and earns strict construction under *Kuhajda*?**

Everything turns on that. If the *Hingson*/*Gorka* reading holds, *Trace Elements* follows almost mechanically. If it does not, (c)(3) is a purely procedural detail and *Kuhajda* should have governed the outcome.

***Wheaton* remains the genuine outlier**, and the *Trace Elements* majority's silence about it is still worth pressing. *Wheaton*'s alternative holding applies the substantive-compliance test to excuse noncompliance with a *different* rule set (2.516), which is a broader move than *Kuhajda* made. Whether that survives *Trace Elements* is unresolved.

**Verify next:** *Hingson* (2002) and *Gorka* (2010) — the two cases the whole edifice rests on. Neither has been read.

**A second thread has emerged worth its own section:** the override-and-conform dialogue runs in *both* directions. Anstead, J., in *Nichols* (2006) called on the Legislature to restore the insured's one-way fee street; the Legislature eventually responded by **repealing § 627.428 entirely** in 2023. Legislative responses to this line of cases have not consistently favored claimants, and the memo should not frame the dialogue as a one-way corrective.

### ✅ *Fla. Bar re Amendment to Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 1.442*, 550 So. 2d 442 (Fla. July 27, 1989)

- **Author:** Per curiam — Ehrlich, C.J., Overton, McDonald, Shaw, Barkett, Grimes, Kogan, JJ. **Unanimous.**
- **Origin:** in **1988** the Court asked the Civil Procedure Rules Committee to examine the conflict among §§ 768.79, 45.061, and rule 1.442.
- **What was asked for:** the Committee (20–4) proposed a sanction of **15% of the unaccepted offer** whenever the verdict missed by more than 25%, and urged the Court **to declare §§ 768.79 and 45.061 unconstitutional**. The four-member minority wanted a costs-only sanction. The **Board of Governors rejected** the percentage sanction but **also urged unconstitutionality**.
- **What the Court did:** refused the percentage sanction — a sanction based on a percentage of an offer "will not have the positive effects urged," and "**it is wiser policy to have a sanction based on costs and attorneys fees. This is what the legislature did** in both of the statutes under review, **and this legislative determination is persuasive.**"
- ✅ **Confirms the correction a third time.** "[I]n this nonadversarial petition **we decline to address the constitutionality** of the purely substantive aspects of sections 768.79 and 45.061." Proceeds instead by **supersession**: the statutes "impinge upon this Court's duties in their *procedural* details" — the example given is the acceptance window, **30 days under § 768.79 versus 45 days under § 45.061**. Withdrew rule 1.442 and adopted the replacement **effective 12:01 a.m., January 1, 1990**.
- ⚠️ **Flagged the constitutional question it declined to answer.** A percentage sanction "raises a serious question of whether this Court impinges upon the legislative prerogative … it is not so clear that a sanction is 'procedural' when it imposes a '**fine**' based on a percentage of an unaccepted offer, **especially when a party may have done nothing more serious than guessing wrong about a jury verdict**." *Sarkis* quotes this line fourteen years later.
- ⭐⭐ **The finding that matters for the apportionment line.** The 1990 rule's own form-of-offer provision, (c)(1)–(2), reads:
  > "(1) An offer of judgment **may be made by any party or parties**.
  > (2) The offer … shall **settle all pending claims**; … shall name the **party or parties** making the offer and the **party or parties** to whom the offer is made; … shall state the **total amount** of the offer."
  **The rule immediately preceding the modern one expressly authorized multi-party offers, in the plural, stated as a single total amount, with no apportionment requirement of any kind.** Apportionment enters the rule only in 1996, and only for the *Fabre* reason (below). This is direct textual support for Harding's *Hingson* dissent and against reading a 1990-era offer as requiring apportionment.
- **Reactive to:** the statutes (and the Court's own 1988 referral). **Not** reactive to a decision.
- **Arc:** #1 · #2 · substance/procedure · rule genealogy

### ✅ *In re Amendments to Florida Rules of Civil Procedure*, 682 So. 2d 105 (Fla. Oct. 31, 1996) — ⭐⭐ **the *Fabre* verification**

- The quadrennial report. Rule 1.442 is rewritten here as **Proposals for Settlement**, effective January 1, 1997.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **THE 1996 COMMITTEE NOTE, VERBATIM FROM THE PRIMARY SOURCE (682 So. 2d at 126):**
  > "This rule was amended to reconcile, where possible, sections 44.102(6) (formerly 44.102(5)(b)), 45.061, 73.032, and 768.79, Florida Statutes, and the decisions of the Florida Supreme Court in *Knealing v. Puleo*, 675 So. 2d 593 (Fla. 1996), *TGI Friday's, Inc. v. Dvorak*, 663 So. 2d 606 (Fla. 1995), and *Timmons v. Combs*, 608 So. 2d 1 (Fla. 1992). This rule replaces former rule 1.442, which was repealed by the *Timmons* decision, and supersedes those sections of the Florida Statutes and the prior decisions of the court, where reconciliation is impossible, in order to provide a workable structure for proposing settlements in civil actions. **The provision which requires that a joint proposal state the amount and terms attributable to each party is in order to conform with *Fabre v. Marin*, 623 So.2d 1182 (Fla.1993).**"
- ✅ **Pariente's *Lamb* characterization is confirmed exactly.** The *Fabre* origin of (c)(3) is now **primary-sourced**. Remove the ⚠️ single-source caveat everywhere it appears.
- **(c)(3) as adopted in 1996** — the text that has governed ever since, unchanged in substance for thirty years: "A proposal may be made by or to any party or parties and by or to any combination of parties properly identified in the proposal. A joint proposal shall state the amount and terms attributable to each party."
- ✅ **Confirms the 1992 interim rule text.** Between *Timmons* and 1997, rule 1.442 read in its entirety: "**Parties shall comply with the procedure set forth in section 768.79, Florida Statutes (1991).**" This is the rule version at issue in *Hingson* — it contains no apportionment language whatever, which is why *Hingson* had to derive apportionment from the **statute** alone.
- **Also here:** the Court **rejected** the Committee's and Board's recommendation to add "the entitlement to" and a "whether the proposal was reasonably rejected" factor to (h)(2), because both were "at variance with section 768.79" and *TGI Friday's*. ⭐ An early instance of the Court refusing to let the rule outrun the statute — the mirror image of what *Trace Elements* is accused of.
- **Reactive to:** decisions (*Knealing*, *TGI Friday's*, *Timmons*, *Fabre*) **and** statutes. The only amendment in the chain reactive to both.

### ✅ *Fabre v. Marin*, 623 So. 2d 1182 (Fla. Aug. 26, 1993) — ⭐⭐ read to test the (c)(3) premise

- **Author:** Grimes, J. — Overton, McDonald, Shaw, Harding, JJ., concur. **Barkett, C.J., dissenting, joined by Kogan, J. 5–2.**
- **Holding:** under **§ 768.81(3)**, judgment for noneconomic damages is entered against each party **on the basis of that party's percentage of fault**, and the percentage is computed against **all participants in the accident — including nonparties**. Approves *Messmer* (5th DCA); quashes *Fabre* (3d DCA).
- **Facts:** Ann Marin, injured as a passenger in a car driven by her husband Ramon, sued the Fabres. The jury found Mrs. Fabre and **Mr. Marin** each 50% at fault. Mr. Marin was a **nonparty** — his wife could not sue him because of **interspousal tort immunity** (since abrogated, *Waite v. Waite*, 618 So. 2d 1360 (Fla. 1993), noted in the Court's own n.2).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **What this does to the thesis — three findings.**
  1. ***Fabre* is a defendant-side fault-allocation case.** It apportions *liability among tortfeasors* by percentage of fault. It says nothing about how **co-plaintiffs** divide a lump-sum settlement demand, and nothing about the mechanics of settlement offers at all. A rule provision "in order to conform with *Fabre*" is a provision about making joint proposals reflect **comparative fault** — exactly Pariente's point, now confirmable without relying on her.
  2. ⭐ **The spouse in *Fabre* is on the other side of the "v."** The spouse whose share had to be broken out was an at-fault **nonparty tortfeasor**. In *Hingson*, *D'Angelo*, *Audiffred*, and *Trace Elements* the spouses are **co-claimants with a unified claim and no fault to allocate**. The provision is being applied to the mirror-image situation of the one that produced it. This is the sharpest available form of the "built for a different job" argument.
  3. ⭐⭐ **The same word, in the same session law, construed in opposite directions.** *Fabre* construes "**party**" in § 768.81(3); *Hingson* construes "**party**" in § 768.79(2)(b). **Both sections were enacted by the Tort Reform and Insurance Act of 1986, ch. 86-160** (*Fabre* says so expressly of § 768.81; the § 768.79 credit line begins s. 58, ch. 86-160). In *Fabre* the Court held "party" was **"not intended as a word of limitation"** and refused to confine it to litigants — quoting *Messmer* with approval: "Had the legislature intended the apportionment computation to be limited to … those who happened to be parties to the proceeding, **it would have so stated**." In *Hingson* the Court made the singular "party" **precisely** a word of limitation. ⚠️ Verify the ch. 86-160 section number for § 768.81 before deploying this.
- **Method note:** *Fabre* is emphatically textualist — "We conclude that the statute is **unambiguous**. By its clear terms …" — and rejects the district court's ambiguity finding. It reaches legislative purpose only in the alternative. Consistent with *Coates*; in tension with *Hingson*'s two hedged sentences.
- **Arc:** #3 joint-proposal apportionment (origin) · #2 interpretive method
