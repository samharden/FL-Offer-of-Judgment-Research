# legislative-strategy-insurer/

**Created August 7, 2026.**

An advocacy planning exercise built on top of the § 768.79 research project: **how would a medical malpractice insurer amend Fla. Stat. § 768.79 to improve its own outcomes, and would it survive the Florida Supreme Court?**

⚠️ **This folder is one-sided by design.** The parent project (`memo.md`) is a neutral survey of the regime. Everything here is written from the carrier's chair. Part 7 of the strategy memo states the case against, and Part 8 states what is unverified. **Do not let this material migrate into the survey.**

## The three findings that carry the memo

1. ⭐ **§ 768.79 is the caps substitute.** ⚠️ The Legislature's two attempts to limit medical malpractice recovery through damages caps were struck (*McCall* 2014, *Kalitan* 2017) on **equal protection** grounds. A fee-shifting consequence classifies by **litigation conduct**, not claimant identity — and *McCall*'s identified defect was precisely an arbitrary allocation by claimant identity.

2. ⭐ **The Legislature can disarm rule 1.442(c)(3) without touching the rule.** *Trace Elements* sustains (c)(3) by holding it implements the substantive requirement *Hingson* found in the singular "party" of § 768.79(2)(b). Amend (2)(b) and (c)(3) implements nothing substantive — at which point *Kuhajda*'s tail-wagging-the-dog holding does the rest.

3. 🔴 **Article I, § 26 is the sleeper, and it exists only in medical malpractice.** ⚠️ The 2004 amendment guarantees a med-mal claimant 70%/90% of damages recovered. A fee award against a partially-successful claimant can breach that floor. Nothing in this project's corpus addresses it. The mitigation — a non-recourse award where the claimant obtains a judgment — is also the cheapest political concession available.
