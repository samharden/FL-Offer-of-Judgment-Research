### ⭐ Three findings the matrix produces on its own

**1. The apportionment arc is the thinnest of the major arcs, and the most fractured.** Eight primary authorities, of which the two that set the rule — *Hingson* and *Trace Elements* — are both **4–3**, and two more (*Pratt*, *Audiffred*) are 5–2 over dissents that never reach the merits. Compare arc 1 (16 authorities, mostly lopsided) and arc 4 (8 authorities, *Nichols* 5–2 but stable since). **The arc carrying the most doctrinal weight has the weakest majorities behind it.**

**2. Arc 6 (good faith) is the corpus's real gap.** Five entries, and **four of them are asides**: *Leapai* n.2's 1992 doubt about "a one dollar offer," *Frosti*'s unchallenged $1 punitive offer, *Anderson*'s note that motive goes to good faith rather than validity, and *Coates*'s reliance on the § 768.79(8)(b) factors to police frivolous appeals. **No read case squarely decides a good-faith challenge.** Given that good faith is the *only* escape hatch from an otherwise mechanical entitlement (*TGI Friday's* / *Schmidt*), this is a hole worth naming in the memo — and possibly the most promising unexplored question in the whole project.

**3. Vote margin tracks subject matter, not era.** The 4–3s cluster in one arc: *Hingson* (3), *Gorka* (4/3), *Allen* (4/3), *Trace Elements* (3), plus *Rollins* (a PIP case). Deadlines, scope, retroactivity, and calculation produce near-unanimity. **The Court is not generally divided about § 768.79 — it is divided about apportionment and ambiguity specifically.**

### ⭐ What the district data shows

**The Second District is the Court's preferred authority in this field, and the Fourth is its most frequent corrective target.** The 2d is net +5 across 28 dispositions; the 3d is net −5 on only nine appearances, four of them quashals.

**The 1-versus-3 pattern is real and confined to one arc.** Three times the Court has adopted a **single district's minority position against three others** — *Hingson* (2002), *Sarkis* (2003), *Audiffred* (2015). Two of the three are apportionment cases. ⚠️ *Sarkis* is a fee-calculation case, so the pattern is "minority-siding in the strict-construction family," slightly broader than apportionment alone. **State it that way; don't overclaim.**

**And the pattern inverts exactly once — in *Wheaton* (2019)**, where the Court sided with *three* districts against the 3d, and did so to *excuse* noncompliance. The only case in the corpus where the Court joined the majority of districts in this family is also the corpus's leading anti-strict-construction decision. That is not a coincidence worth asserting as causation, but it is worth putting on the page.

⚠️ **Limits of this matrix.** It counts **Supreme Court dispositions**, not district-court outcomes. It shows which districts the Court agrees with, not how the districts actually rule across the ~816 DCA opinions — that requires the DCA corpus and the four-signal cascade. **Do not present this as the district-split matrix contemplated in the research plan; it is the *Supreme Court's* alignment matrix.** Label it accordingly in the memo.

### ⭐ What the timeline shows that the case law alone does not

**Three of the four decision-driven rulemaking events were corrections of the Court's own work** — 1992 (*Timmons* repeals the rule), 2010 ((c)(4) undoes *Lamb*), 2022 (conforming to the Legislature's undoing of *Gorka*). **The rule has been amended to fix a decision more often than to implement one.**

**The 1996 rewrite is the only amendment that responds to both tracks at once** — and it is the one that produced (c)(3).

**The Legislature has touched § 768.79 four times in forty years** (1986, 1990, 1997 reviser's, 2022) and **substantively only three**. Against that, the Court has amended the rule at least eight times. ⚠️ *ch. 87-249 and ch. 99-225 remain unresolved and could change this count — Phase 1.*

**The 2003 → 2005 → 2010 loop is the timeline's centerpiece.** Committee proposes fix → Court refuses, citing its own precedent → Court creates exactly that problem → Court adopts the fix five years later. **This is documented, not inferred**, and *Trace Elements* has now set it up to run a third time.

**The "civil actions for damages" defect has an open 34-year lifespan.** Flagged by commenters in 1992, called "beyond this Court's control because substantive," litigated in *Nichols* (2006) and *Diamond Aircraft* (2013), resolved **by construction** both times, and **never amended**. The Court asked the Legislature to act in 1992 and the Legislature has not.

## 4. What this changes for the memo

1. **Lead the diagnostic section with the matrix, not the anecdote.** The 4–3s cluster in one arc. That is a structural claim, not an impression.
2. **Name the good-faith gap.** Arc 6 has no squarely decided case in the read corpus, and it is the only discretionary escape from mechanical entitlement.
3. **Relabel the district work.** What exists is the **Supreme Court's alignment matrix**. The true district-split matrix still requires the DCA corpus.
4. **The 1-vs-3 pattern is "minority-siding in the strict-construction family,"** not apportionment alone — *Sarkis* is a fee-calculation case. State it precisely.
5. **Use the timeline for the repudiation argument**, not the case list — the 2003/2005/2010 loop and the 2025/2026 sequence are far stronger in chronological form.
