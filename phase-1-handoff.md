## 2. The assignment

Three questions, in priority order.

### Q1. Resolve ch. 87-249 — does it exist, and did it amend § 768.79?

**The problem.** Secondary sources routinely describe ch. 87-249, Laws of Florida (1987), as having rewritten or substantially amended § 768.79. But the statute's own credit line does not mention it.

**The credit line, verified from the current statute:**

> s. 58, ch. 86-160; s. 48, ch. 90-119; s. 1175, ch. 97-102; s. 24, ch. 2022-271.

**What we need:** whether ch. 87-249 touched § 768.79 at all; if so, what it did and why it is absent from the credit line; if not, where the widespread description came from. Either answer is useful. A clean negative is a real finding.

**Note the possible confound:** **§ 45.061 was enacted in 1987**, a year after § 768.79. It is plausible that ch. 87-249 relates to § 45.061 (or to another part of the 1986–87 tort reform sequence) and has been misattributed. Check that before concluding anything.

### Q2. Did ch. 99-225 touch § 768.79?

Ch. 99-225 is the 1999 Tort Reform Act, later **invalidated on single-subject grounds**. We need to know whether it amended § 768.79 before it fell.

Because it was invalidated, it will not appear in the current credit line either way — so the credit line cannot answer this. You need the session law itself.

If it did amend the statute, note **what** it changed and **what happened on invalidation** (whether the prior text revived).

### Q3. Pull the 1990 staff analysis — and settle the chamber discrepancy

This is the **highest-value document** in the assignment and the hardest to get.

Two Florida Supreme Court opinions cite what appears to be the same document, and they disagree about which chamber produced it:

| Source | Citation as given |
|---|---|
| *White v. Steak and Ale of Florida, Inc.*, 816 So. 2d 546 (Fla. 2002), majority, n.1 | "Fla. S. Comm. on Insurance, **CS for SB 2670** Staff Analysis 7 (May 24, 1990)" |
| *White*, Harding, J., dissenting in part | "**CS/HB 2670** (1990) Staff Analysis 7 (May 24, 1990)" |

Same date, same page. One of them is wrong.

**What we need:** the actual staff analysis. Which chamber, correct bill number, and the document itself. It is the best pre-2010 primary source identified anywhere in this project, and it is the legislative history for ch. 90-119 — the most consequential amendment in the statute's history.

**Expect this may not be online.** See § 5 on sources. If it is not retrievable remotely, the correct deliverable is **a precise location and a route to obtain it** — repository, collection, call number, and who to contact — not a reconstruction.

### Q4 (flagged, lower priority) — the ch. 86-160 section number for § 768.81

We know § 768.79 was enacted at **s. 58, ch. 86-160**. We know from *Fabre v. Marin*, 623 So. 2d 1182 (Fla. 1993), that **§ 768.81 was enacted as part of the same act** — the Tort Reform and Insurance Act of 1986. We do not have § 768.81's **section number within ch. 86-160**.

⚠️ **Treat anything you find here as provisional.** This item feeds a substantive argument in the project's thesis and will be independently re-verified on our side before use. Report what you find; do not treat it as settled.

## 3. What is already verified — do not re-derive

This is established from primary sources or from opinions construing them. Use it; don't spend time reproducing it.

**Ch. 90-119 (1990), effective October 1, 1990 — fully sourced.**
- **§ 48** rewrote § 768.79(1) and **added subsection (6)**. Its effect: a **defendant** could now recover where the judgment is "one of no liability." Before 1990 the statute measured against "the judgment obtained **by the plaintiff**," so defendants could not recover on a defense verdict.
- **§ 22** repealed **§ 45.061** as to causes of action accruing after October 1, 1990.
- Sourced from: *Timmons v. Combs*, 608 So. 2d 1 (Fla. 1992); *MX Investments v. Crawford*, 700 So. 2d 640 (Fla. 1997) (quoting the First District); *Knealing v. Puleo*, 675 So. 2d 593 (Fla. 1996) n.5; *Gulliver Academy v. Bodek*, 694 So. 2d 675 (Fla. 1997) n.1.

**§ 45.061 — full picture.** Enacted **1987**, a year *after* § 768.79. Repealed **prospectively** in 1990. Survives for pre-October-1990 causes of action and remains on the books — cited as § 45.061, Fla. Stat. (2020) in a 2021 opinion.

**Ch. 2022-271, § 24 (2022).** Added a new **§ 768.79(6)** and **renumbered the subsections**. Current numbering, verified from the statute: (1) entitlement · (2) offer requirements · (3) service and filing · (4) acceptance · (5) withdrawal · (6) property-insurer conditional joint offers · (7) post-judgment cost-and-fee determination · (8) good faith and reasonableness factors.

**Ch. 2023-15 (HB 837), § 11** repealed **§ 627.428**, effective March 24, 2023. It did **not** amend § 768.79.

**Origin.** § 768.79 enacted 1986 by ch. 86-160 § 58 (Tort Reform and Insurance Act). § 768.81 (comparative fault) enacted by the same act.

## 4. Traps — read before starting

🔴 **There are two different subsection (6)s. This will bite you immediately.**

The **(6) added in 1990** is the judgment-comparison provision — the "no liability" language and the 25% arithmetic. After ch. 2022-271 inserted a *new* (6) for property-insurer conditional joint offers, **the 1990 provision became (7)**.

So "§ 768.79(6)" means the judgment-comparison provision in any **pre-2022** source and the property-insurer carve-out in any **post-2022** source. **Always tie a subsection reference to a year.** Any document you produce should state which version it is describing.

⚠️ **Different temporal triggers for the two statutes.** § 768.79's applicable version is the one in effect **when the cause of action accrued** (§ 768.71(2) limits the part to causes arising on or after July 1, 1986). § 45.061's trigger is **the making of the offer**. Do not assume a shared rule.

⚠️ **Terminology shifted in 1996.** Pre-1996 materials say "offer of judgment" or "demand for judgment." Post-1996 they say "proposal for settlement." A search on the modern phrase will silently miss everything before 1997. Search on the statute number.

⚠️ **Three instruments governed simultaneously before 1996** — § 45.061, § 768.79, and rule 1.442. A 1990-era document may be discussing any of them. Confirm which before attributing it.

## 5. Sources, and what does and doesn't work

**Known not to work:**
- **LegiScan.** Florida coverage begins in **2010**. It cannot see ch. 86-160, 87-249, 90-119, or 97-102. Do not spend time here for anything pre-2010.
- **flsenate.gov year archive** — goes back only to **1998**. Useful for ch. 99-225, useless for 1987 and 1990.

**Where to actually look:**
- **Laws of Florida** — `laws.flrules.org`. Session laws by chapter number. This is the primary route for Q1 and Q2.
- **Florida State Archives** (Tallahassee) — legislative records, committee files, staff analyses. The likely home of the 1990 staff analysis.
- **FSU College of Law Research Center** — maintains a Florida legislative history collection and a documented research guide. Second-best route for Q3, and the most likely source of a usable finding aid.
- **University of Florida Levin College of Law** — also holds Florida legislative materials.
- **Florida Legislature's Division of Legislative Information Services** — can sometimes retrieve historical bill files on request.

## 6. Deliverable format — this part matters

**Produce documents, not descriptions.**

For each question, return one of:

1. **The document itself** — PDF, scan, or a stable URL to it — plus a one-paragraph statement of what it establishes; or
2. **A precise retrieval route** — repository, collection name, box/folder or call number, and contact — if the document exists but could not be obtained remotely; or
3. **A documented negative** — what you searched, where, and why you conclude the thing does not exist or does not say what it is reputed to say.

**All three of those are successful outcomes.** A documented negative on ch. 87-249 is genuinely valuable — it closes an open question that has been carried in this project's notes for weeks.

**What is not acceptable as a deliverable:** a summary of what a document probably says, a citation reconstructed from secondary sources, or a paraphrase presented without a locatable source. Archival legislative history is precisely the area where plausible-sounding but unverifiable citations do the most damage, because they are hard for the next reader to check. If you cannot verify it, say so — that is a useful answer and will be treated as one.

**Flag your confidence.** For each finding, state whether you saw the primary document, saw a secondary source describing it, or are inferring.

## 7. Conventions used in this project

Adopt these so the returned work drops straight in:

- **Absolute dates everywhere.** Never "last year" or "recently."
- **Every statutory pin cite carries a year** — e.g. "§ 768.79(6) (2019)" or "§ 768.79(7) (2024)." Non-negotiable given the renumbering.
- **Session laws cited as** `ch. 90-119, § 48, Laws of Fla.`
- **Mark unverified claims explicitly.** The project uses ⚠️ for "needs verification" and ✅ for "verified from a primary source." Keeping that distinction visible is more useful than a confident-sounding paragraph.
- **Distinguish what a document says from what a court said it says.** Much of what we currently "know" about ch. 90-119 comes from opinions describing it. Where you have the session law itself, say so — that is an upgrade in provenance and we want to record it as one.
