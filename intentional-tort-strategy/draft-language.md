### The three definitional handles already in Florida law

Do not invent a definition of "intentional tort." Three exist, each already litigated:

| Handle | Text | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **§ 768.72(2)(a)** | "intentional misconduct" — the defendant had "actual knowledge of the wrongfulness of the conduct and the high probability that injury or damage to the claimant would result" and deliberately proceeded | ⭐ **The primary handle.** In chapter 768, tied to a judicial screening motion |
| **§ 768.735(1)** | "any civil action based upon child abuse, abuse of the elderly under chapter 415, or abuse of the developmentally disabled" | ⭐ **The precedent.** Already exempts this class from §§ 768.72(2)–(4), 768.725, and 768.73 |
| **§ 95.11(8), (10)** | actions founded on abuse as defined in §§ 39.01, 415.102, 984.03; incest under § 826.04; violations of § 794.011; actions under § 787.061 | The enumerated-offense fallback if the class must be narrowed |

# Scenario A — The Nominal Offer Fix

## A-1. The good-faith floor

Amend § 768.79(8)(a):

> (8)(a) If a party is entitled to costs and fees pursuant to the provisions of this section, the court may, in its discretion, determine that an offer was not made in good faith. In such case, the court may disallow an award of costs and attorney's fees.
>
> **In an action in which punitive damages are sought, or which is based upon intentional misconduct as defined in s. 768.72(2)(a), an offer is presumed not to have been made in good faith if:**
>
> **1. The offer purports to resolve a claim for punitive damages for a nominal amount; or**
>
> **2. The offer was not accompanied by a written statement of the factual basis for the amount offered.**
>
> **A party may overcome this presumption by a preponderance of the evidence. This paragraph does not limit the court's discretion to determine that any other offer was not made in good faith.**

**Drafting notes.**

- ⭐ **Why "nominal" and not a percentage.** A percentage floor ("less than 10% of claimed damages") invites the offeror to argue about the denominator and rewards inflated ad damnum pleading. "Nominal" is a judicial concept the courts already apply, and it targets the actual practice — the $1 punitive proposal that *Frosti* left unexamined.
- ⚠️ **Consider defining "nominal."** A safe-harbour figure (e.g. the greater of $500 or 1% of the amount offered on the compensatory claim) makes the provision self-executing but hands opponents a number to attack. **Have both versions.** The undefined version is the opening ask.
- ⭐ **Paragraph 2 is the sleeper and it is nearly free.** It imposes no ceiling on anyone's offer; it requires only that the offeror write down why. An offeror acting in good faith can always satisfy it, which makes it very hard to speak against — and it manufactures, in every case, the contemporaneous record that a later good-faith challenge needs.
- **Preponderance, not clear and convincing.** The carrier-side draft asks for clear and convincing on its competing presumption. Matching that escalation makes the provision look like what it is — a positional fight — and preponderance captures most of the value.
- **Art. V:** good faith is a creature of the statute, at (8)(a), and it determines when the substantive right to fees exists. Legislating its standard legislates entitlement. Clean.
- 🔴 **This provision is in a race.** See [`strategy-memo.md`](strategy-memo.md) Part 7.1.

## A-2. The punitive-gate provision

Create § 768.79(9):

> **(9) No entitlement to costs or attorney's fees arises under this section from an offer served at a time when a motion under s. 768.72 was pending and undetermined in the action, or served before such a motion was filed if the motion is granted within the period allowed for acceptance of the offer. This subsection is a limitation on the circumstances in which the substantive right created by this section arises and is not a requirement as to the time at which an offer may be served.**

**Drafting notes.**

- 🔴 **The last sentence is not decoration. It is the whole provision.** *Knealing* struck § 44.102(6) because the Legislature tried to regulate proposal *timing* through a statute; timing is rule 1.442(b)'s territory and is procedural. **This provision must never say when a proposal may be served.** It says only when entitlement arises — which is what § 768.79 exists to say.
- **Why the second clause.** Without it, a defendant serves a proposal the day before the § 768.72 motion is filed and the provision does nothing. The 30-day acceptance window under rule 1.442(f)(1) is the natural period to look through.
- ⚠️ **Interaction with rule 1.442(f)(1).** The rule's 30-day period is procedural and this provision must not appear to alter it. Phrase it as "the period allowed for acceptance," referring to whatever that period is, rather than reciting "30 days."
- ⚠️ **Consider a narrower fallback:** limit the provision to offers that purport to resolve a punitive-damages claim. Narrower, easier to defend, and reaches the abuse this is actually aimed at.

## A-3. Codify the comparator

Amend § 768.79(7), adding at the end of the "judgment obtained" definition:

> **For purposes of this subsection, "judgment obtained" includes any punitive damages awarded.**

**Drafting notes.**

- ⭐ **Low risk, and it settles a question the Court has twice declined to reach.** In *CCM Condominium* (2021) the Fourth District held below that the plain meaning of § 768.79 does **not** support the *White* formula; the Supreme Court applied *State v. Poole*'s "clearly erroneous" standard and declined to examine it. Canady, C.J., dissenting, argued the formula's supporting authorities were § 627.428 cases with "no relevant textual similarity." **The formula is unexamined and, on the current bench, not obviously safe.** Codifying the piece that matters to this constituency removes it from the argument.
- ⚠️ **Confirm the current state of the law first.** This may already be the rule. If it is, the provision is a clarifying amendment and should be labelled as one in the bill analysis — a "clarifying" tag is worth real votes and it forecloses the argument that the Legislature changed the law by implication elsewhere.
- **Art. V:** defines a term in the statute's own comparison provision. Clean.

# Scenario B — The Intentional Misconduct Carve-Out

## B-1 — Full exclusion *(recommended)*

Amend § 768.79(1), first sentence:

> (1) In any civil action for damages filed in the courts of this state, **other than an action described in subsection (10),** if a defendant files an offer of judgment which is not accepted by the plaintiff within 30 days …

Create § 768.79(10):

> **(10)(a) This section does not apply to a claim for damages in an action in which the court has granted a motion under s. 768.72 permitting a claim for punitive damages against a defendant on the basis of intentional misconduct as defined in s. 768.72(2)(a). No party has an entitlement to costs or attorney's fees under this section as to such a claim, and any offer previously served as to such a claim confers no entitlement.**
>
> **(b) Paragraph (a) applies to a defendant against whom such a motion has been granted, including a defendant whose liability is established under s. 768.72(3).**
>
> **(c) The court may, on motion of any party and on a showing that the claim is based upon conduct described in s. 768.72(2)(a), enter an order applying paragraph (a) to a claim for which punitive damages are not sought. An order under this paragraph is subject to the same showing required by s. 768.72(1).**
>
> **(d) This subsection applies to any offer served on or after [effective date], regardless of when the cause of action accrued.**

**Drafting notes.**

- ⭐ **(a) is drafted around a judicial order, not a pleading.** That is the answer to the pleading-manipulation objection ([`strategy-memo.md`](strategy-memo.md) Part 5). A plaintiff cannot claim the exemption by adding a count; a judge must grant leave on an evidentiary showing under § 768.72(1).
- ⭐ **(b) is the provision that reaches the institutional defendant**, which is the one that actually serves proposals. Section 768.72(3) supplies the corporate standard — active participation, knowing condonation, or gross negligence contributing to the loss. **Without (b), the carve-out reaches only the perpetrator and is worth very little.**
- **(c) is the escape valve for claimants who do not plead punitive damages.** Without it, the bill forces a punitive claim into every case that wants the exemption, which is a bad outcome and an easy attack. Note that (c) borrows § 768.72(1)'s standard so it creates no new evidentiary test.
- 🔴 **(d) is the most legally exposed sentence in this appendix.** *Jones Boatyard* keys the applicable version of § 768.79 to **accrual**, via § 768.71(2). This clause sets a different trigger for a new subsection. **The Legislature has used a proposal-date trigger before — § 45.061's operative event was the making of the offer (*Leapai*) — so the drafting is not unprecedented, but its validity against § 768.71(2) is unresearched.** See [`strategy-memo.md`](strategy-memo.md) Gate 4 and Part 10. **Draft it explicitly; do not leave it to construction. And sever it**, so that a successful challenge to (d) leaves (a)–(c) operating prospectively rather than taking the subsection down.
- ⚠️ **"as to such a claim."** In a case pleading both intentional and negligence counts against different defendants, the carve-out should follow the claim, not the case. Otherwise a single § 768.72 order switches § 768.79 off for a co-defendant who did nothing intentional — which is both unfair and an easy target. **Confirm this works mechanically against the *Frosti* rule that the whole net judgment is compared, not a favourable slice of it.** This interaction is not fully worked out and is the most likely drafting defect in this appendix.

## B-2 — One-way *(alternative; not recommended)*

Substitute for B-1(a):

> **(10)(a) A defendant has no entitlement to costs or attorney's fees under this section as to a claim described in this subsection, and any offer served by such a defendant confers no entitlement. The entitlement of a claimant under this section is not affected.**

**Drafting notes.**

- **Precedent exists, in this posture, in Florida.** § 772.11 makes a claimant's fee recovery turn on prevailing, but a **defendant's** recovery turn on "a finding that the claimant raised a claim that was without substantial fact or legal support" — an asymmetric standard, already enacted, for a victim-type claim. § 787.061 provides fees to a prevailing victim with **no prevailing-defendant provision at all**.
- 🔴 **But the political headwind is direct.** Ch. 2023-15 § 11 repealed § 627.428 — Florida's most prominent one-way fee shift — effective March 24, 2023 (*Coates* n.2). Asking this Legislature for a new one-way shift is asking it to reverse its own signature reform. **B-2 raises the constitutional risk very little and the political risk very much.** That is why B-1 is recommended.
- If B-2 is filed anyway, file it as an amendment to B-1 rather than as the bill, so that the fallback is already drafted when it is stripped.

# Scenario C — The Convicted Offender Bar

## C-1. The bar

Create § 768.79(11):

> **(11)(a) A defendant who has been convicted of, or who has pled guilty or nolo contendere to, a criminal offense arising from the same act, transaction, or occurrence that is the basis of a claim in a civil action has no entitlement to costs or attorney's fees under this section as against a person who was a victim of that offense, and any offer served by that defendant confers no entitlement. This paragraph applies whether or not adjudication was withheld.**
>
> **(b) Paragraph (a) applies if the conviction or plea occurs at any time before an award under this section becomes final. On motion filed within 60 days after the conviction or plea, the court shall vacate any award of costs or attorney's fees entered in favor of the defendant, and shall retain jurisdiction for that purpose.**

**Drafting notes.**

- **"Whether or not adjudication was withheld" is not optional.** A nolo plea with adjudication withheld is the ordinary disposition in many of these prosecutions. A provision limited to "convicted" would exclude a large share of the intended class and would generate litigation about which dispositions count.
- ⭐ **(b) is what makes the provision work.** Criminal cases resolve on their own schedule and frequently after the civil case. Without a look-back and a retained-jurisdiction clause, the bar misses most of the cases it is written for.
- ⚠️ **Test (b) against rule 1.525 and *Saia*.** *Saia Motor Freight* holds rule 1.525's 30-day period is a bright line and that a reservation of jurisdiction in the final judgment does not extend it. **A statutory retention of jurisdiction to vacate an award is not the same thing as a reservation to enter one, but the distinction has not been tested.** Consider whether (b) needs a conforming rule amendment — and note the irony that this is the one provision in the package that may need the Court's cooperation.
- **"Victim of that offense"** should be defined by cross-reference rather than fresh drafting. § 960.03 and art. I, § 16(b) both supply a definition; pick one and cite it.
- **Art. V:** withdrawal of a statutory entitlement, classified by criminal conviction. The cleanest provision in the appendix.

## C-2. The claimant-side enhancement

Create § 768.79(11)(c):

> **(c) In an action described in paragraph (a), the costs and attorney's fees awarded to the claimant under this section include those incurred on or after the date the claimant served a written demand for damages upon the defendant, provided the demand was served not less than 30 days before the action was filed and stated the amount demanded and the factual basis for it.**

**Drafting notes.**

- ⭐ **Modeled directly on § 772.11**, which already conditions a Florida statutory remedy on a written presuit demand with a 30-day cure period. The template is enacted, familiar, and uncontroversial.
- **This is the provision that makes the package an ask rather than only a shield**, which matters for how it is received.
- ⚠️ It will draw the objection that it rewards a demand made before the defendant has any information. The 30-day period and the requirement to state a factual basis are the answer; **consider adding that the demand must be served after the conviction or plea**, which narrows the provision considerably but makes it nearly unanswerable.

## C-3. The non-recourse provision *(the concession)*

Create § 768.79(11)(d):

> **(d) An award of costs or attorney's fees under this section against a natural person who was the victim of an offense described in paragraph (a) is enforceable only against the proceeds of any judgment obtained by that person in the action, and is not otherwise enforceable against that person.**

**Drafting notes.**

- ⭐ **Offer this early and loudly; do not defend it.** [`strategy-memo.md`](strategy-memo.md) Part 7.3. The carrier-side memo independently proposes the identical mechanism for its own reasons. **A provision both sides will concede can be traded twice — so spend nothing to obtain it.**
- It removes the "this bankrupts victims" fact pattern while leaving the defense-verdict case untouched, because there is no judgment to protect there.
- ⚠️ **Consider whether to extend (d) beyond paragraph (a)'s class**, to any natural-person claimant in an action described in § 768.79(10). Broader, more valuable, and harder to pass — a good candidate for the second bill.

# Cross-cutting checklist

Run every draft against this before filing.

| # | Check | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Does it regulate entitlement, or does it regulate a proposal?** | *Knealing* struck § 44.102(6) for regulating timing. Entitlement survives; content and timing do not. The tell: if the sentence tells a party what to *do*, rewrite it to say what a party is *entitled to*. |
| 2 | **Is every cross-reference checked against the post-2022 numbering?** | Ch. 2022-271 renumbered § 768.79. Old (6) → new (7); old (7)(a)–(b) → new (8)(a)–(b). Two different subsection (6)s exist in the sources. |
| 3 | **Is there an explicit applicability clause?** | *Jones Boatyard* keys the version to accrual. Silence means the amendment reaches no pending case. |
| 4 | **Is the applicability clause severable from the operative text?** | So a retroactivity challenge narrows the bill instead of voiding it. |
| 5 | **Does the class turn on a judicial finding rather than a pleading?** | The pleading-manipulation objection is the one most likely to kill Scenario B. |
| 6 | **Does it reach the institutional co-defendant?** | The perpetrator does not serve proposals. A provision that misses § 768.72(3) defendants misses the money. |
| 7 | **Could the Court undo it by rule within one cycle?** | Anything procedural can be. Under *Kuhajda* and *Trace Elements*, a rule provision implementing nothing substantive is doubly fragile — which is an argument for keeping every provision statutory. |
| 8 | **Does the classification run on conduct rather than claimant identity?** | ⚠️ *McCall*'s identified defect was a burden allocated by family composition. Never classify by who the claimant is. |
| 9 | **Is § 768.735(1) cited in the bill analysis?** | It is the in-chapter precedent for exempting this exact class from part II's damages provisions, and it is the shortest path to "this is consistency, not novelty." |
| 10 | **Do the findings quote the Court on itself?** | *Gorka*, *Anderson* / *Allen*, and Polston, J.'s "inconsistently applied and unpredictable." Judicial diagnosis is the best available rational-basis record. |
