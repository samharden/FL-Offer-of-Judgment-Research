## Q1 - Chapter 87-249

### Finding

✅ **Ch. 87-249 did not touch § 768.79.** It is Committee Substitute for Senate Bill 866. Section 1 created § 45.061, titled "Offers of settlement"; section 2 created § 45.062; section 3 supplied the effective-date provision. The three-page act contains no amendment to, or mention of, § 768.79.

The confound identified in the handoff is therefore real: ch. 87-249 belongs in the history of the parallel **§ 45.061**, not in the credit line for § 768.79. This explains why ch. 87-249 is absent from § 768.79's credit line. The current § 45.061 history line independently identifies `s. 1, ch. 87-249`.

The broader premise that secondary sources "routinely" describe ch. 87-249 as amending § 768.79 was not corroborated in targeted searches. At least one Florida Bar Journal article correctly distinguishes the two enactments, although its footnote appears to contain a section-number typo (`§ 21` rather than `§ 1`). That typo does not change the identity of the statute created.

## Q2 - Chapter 99-225

### Finding

✅ **Ch. 99-225 did not amend or mention § 768.79.** The entire 29-page, 36-section session law was searched for `768.79`, `offer of judgment`, and `offer of settlement`, and its section headings were reviewed. Section 27 amended subsections (3)-(6) of **§ 768.81**, the comparative-fault statute. No section addressed § 768.79.

Accordingly, invalidation of ch. 99-225 created no revival question for § 768.79: the act had made no change to that statute that could fall or revive. This finding does not address the effect of invalidation on § 768.81 or the act's other provisions, which was unnecessary to answer the assigned question.

## Q3 - May 24, 1990 staff analysis

### Finding

✅ **The cited document is a Senate document for CS/SB 2670.** Its first page states:

- `SENATE STAFF ANALYSIS AND ECONOMIC IMPACT STATEMENT`;
- date: May 24, 1990;
- legislative document number: `CS/SB 2670`;
- bill and sponsor: `CS/SB 2670 by Insurance and Senator Langley`.

The first page also bears a reproduction stamp from the Florida State Archives. The copy was located in the appendix to the respondents' answer brief in *MX Investments, Inc. v. Crawford*, Florida Supreme Court No. 89,623, preserved in FSU Law's Florida Supreme Court docket archive.

**Discrepancy resolved:** the majority's citation in *White v. Steak and Ale of Florida, Inc.*, 816 So. 2d 546, 549 n.1 (Fla. 2002), to a Florida Senate committee analysis of CS for SB 2670 is correct in substance. Harding, J.'s reference to `CS/HB 2670` is a miscitation. The enacted chapter independently identifies itself as **Committee Substitute for Senate Bill No. 2670**.

### What the analysis establishes

Page 6 describes then-section 41 as an extensive rewording of the law governing offers of judgment and demands for judgment. Page 7 explains that the provision sought to combine §§ 45.061 and 768.79, identifies its 1989 predecessor, and flags the draft's failure to repeal § 45.061 as a source of continued confusion.

The May 24 draft used **section 41**. The enacted law used **section 48** for the § 768.79 rewrite and **section 22** to make § 45.061 inapplicable to later-accruing causes of action. The difference reflects changes between the May 24 committee substitute and the enrolled act; it is not a conflict in the identity of the bill.

## Q4 - Section number for § 768.81 in ch. 86-160

### Finding

✅ **Section 60 of ch. 86-160 created § 768.81.** The same official pages show that section 58 created § 768.79, section 59 created § 768.80, and section 60 created § 768.81. The project's same-session-law premise is therefore confirmed from the primary act and is no longer provisional.
