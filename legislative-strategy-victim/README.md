# legislative-strategy-victim/

**Created August 7, 2026.**

An advocacy planning exercise built on the § 768.79 research project: **how would the Legislature amend Fla. Stat. § 768.79 to reduce the fee-shifting exposure of victims of intentional torts, and would it survive the Florida Supreme Court?**

⚠️ **This folder is one-sided by design.** It is the **mirror image** of [`../legislative-strategy-insurer/`](../legislative-strategy-insurer/), which argues the same statute from the medical malpractice carrier's chair. The parent project ([`../memo.md`](../memo.md)) is the neutral survey. Part 9 of the strategy memo states the case against; Part 10 states what is unverified. **Do not let this material migrate into the survey.**

## The four findings that carry the memo

1. ⭐ **The Court has already said this clause is the Legislature's to fix.** In the *Timmons* Supplemental Order (Oct. 22, 1992), the Court called § 768.79's "civil actions for damages" limitation "**beyond this Court's control because [it is] substantive**" and referred the matter out. The defect has been resolved by construction twice — *Nichols* (2006), *Diamond Aircraft* (2013) — and **amended never, in thirty-four years**. A scope amendment is therefore squarely substantive, responsive to an express judicial invitation, and **unamendable by rule**.

2. ⭐ **Chapter 768 already contains the carve-out this bill extends.** § 768.735(1), verbatim: *"Sections 768.72(2)-(4), 768.725, and 768.73 do not apply to any civil action based upon child abuse, abuse of the elderly under chapter 415, or abuse of the developmentally disabled."* Same chapter, same part, same class of conduct. Scenario B is that decision extended one section over.

3. ⭐ **The statute's arithmetic runs on a number Florida law forbids the claimant to have.** § 768.72(1) bars pleading punitive damages without a judicial finding of a reasonable evidentiary basis. Rule 1.442(c)(2)(E) lets a defendant price the punitive claim at a nominal figure — *Frosti* (2008) involved **$1** — and arc 6, good faith, is the emptiest arc in the corpus with no squarely decided case in forty years. **A proposal served before the § 768.72 ruling asks the claimant to value a claim it is not yet permitted to assert.**

4. 🔴 **Retroactivity is the sleeper, and it does not appear in the carrier memo.** *Jones Boatyard* keys the applicable version of § 768.79 to **accrual**, via § 768.71(2). Intentional-tort claims have Florida's longest tails — §§ 95.11(8) and (10), the latter with **no limitations period** for § 794.011 claims where the victim was under 16. **The claimants with the most exposure are the ones a 2027 amendment reaches last.** Whether a proposal-date applicability clause survives § 768.71(2) is unresearched and is the leading open question in the memo.

## The structural inversion worth noting

In the carrier-side memo, the largest ask carried the largest constitutional risk. **Here the constitutional odds are the highest in either folder and the entire risk is political.** Withdrawing a statutory entitlement is the least vulnerable thing the Legislature can do to § 768.79 — nothing is abolished, so *Kluger* and article I, § 21 are not in play, and *Knealing* holds that the entitlement is substantive in the first place. The effort belongs in the committee process and the coalition, not in the constitutional briefing.
